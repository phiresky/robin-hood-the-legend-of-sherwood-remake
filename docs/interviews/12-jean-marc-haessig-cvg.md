We all know Robin Hood. He's that legendary English rebel who spoke with an American accent. He resided in Sherwood Forest along with his band of Merry Men, robbing from the rich, giving to the poor, and getting into Maid Marian's knickers on the odd occasion. And he won a silver arrow at some point along the way because he was such a top archer.

Spellbound, which was responsible for the Wild West-themed Desperados, is working on a similarly-styled game based around Big Robin. The title will be set in 12th Century England, and will follow the exploits of Hood and his crew as they try to gather enough gold to ensure the safe return of the imprisoned King Richard. It's a mission-based, real-time tactical affair where players will be sneaking into castles, leaping onto unsuspecting taxmen and rescuing captives from the clutches of villains.

We managed to spot Lincoln green-clad Jean-Marc Haessig recently while journeying through Sherwood Forest, so we collared the creative director of Robin Hood to find out more about the game.

**What's Robin Hood all about? What's the idea behind the game, what is the player required to do, and what is the plot?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** Robin Hood will be a real-time tactics game like Desperados and Commandos, taking place in 12th Century England. The idea is to provide fans of real-time tactics games an alternative to the World War 2 setting of other games of this genre. And though Robin Hood is a famous character throughout the world, there hadn't been many games that dealt with his legend in the past.

The player will have to do a lot of things. Sneaking into mighty castles, ambushing taxmen in the Sherwood Forest, figuring out strategies to solve the missions (including who will join the team in a mission) and so on. The plot will be based on the legend of Robin Hood. The main objective of the game is to gather enough gold for the safe return of King Richard.

**Why have you decided to do a game based around Robin Hood and what sort of research have you done for the title so far?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** Robin Hood and his Merry Men are the ideal characters for a real-time tactics game. His name is known by virtually everyone around the world, and almost everybody knows of at least some of the tales that are told about this character. They're a small band of "merry men" in a struggle against the evil Prince John Lackland and his minions. Robin is not a great leader of a large army inside the Sherwood Forrest who fights an all out war against the Normans. His goals and methods are quite subtle – stealing the taxes from Prince John, giving back the money and saving some of it for King Richard's ransom. And he achieves all of that with a small band of Merry Men. Then again, would Robin Hood have succeeded if he hadn't been accompanied by his Merry Men? If he couldn't count on the strength of Little John or the faith, and beer, of Friar Tuck, or the love of Maid Marian? We think what makes the legend of Robin Hood so imaginable and popular is the various, really strong characters it tells about.

So when Wanadoo Edition approached us with their concept of a Robin Hood game, we both put our ideas together and very soon had a game to show.

Research is always a big issue when designing a new game. There had been a lot of things to learn – for the graphic artists as well as for our level and story designers. Our sources include the Internet, books on Robin Hood, medieval architecture and clothing, film adaptations like "The Adventures of Robin Hood" with Errol Flynn and last but not least Strasbourg, the town in our neighbourhood. Its medieval town centre offered quite a few inspirations for our 3D-modellers who designed the houses and castles in Robin Hood.

Finally, the cities and castles in Robin Hood are initially based on original maps and pictures of York, Derby, Nottingham, Leicester and Lincoln, but have been strongly adapted to our gameplay priorities.

**We understand that the game will be mission-based. Could you give us some idea of the missions that players will undertake, and what locales these will be set in?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** The missions will be set in towns and castles surrounding Sherwood Forrest: Nottingham, York, Lincoln, Leicester and Derby and of course in Sherwood itself. All in all there will be nine places to visit, some of them more than just once and with different ambiance backgrounds (day, night, fog, snow etc.).

What you can expect are quite lot different mission types – from rescuing a captured man to ambushes in the Sherwood Forrest to big castle sieges, where Robin Hood has to reach some strategic goals before the attack begins. The most important thing is that we leave most of the choices to the player. It begins with the tutorials in Robin Hood's camp in Sherwood and does not end in choosing the men who will form the group for the missions.

**How many people will there be in a squad at any one time, and how will players control this squad?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** There will be up to five people forming a group. The controls are similar to those in Desperados and other RTS titles. By point-and-click, the player guides Robin Hood and his men through the map. Each time he can perform an action or he finds an interactive item the cursor will turn into a corresponding icon. This very intuitive kind of interface was a great bonus in Desperados.

Because most of the fighting in Robin Hood is hand-to-hand combat rather then gunfights, we decided to include a fighting system which allows more than just point-and-click on the enemy. By drawing a symbol with his mouse the player-characters will be able to do some nice tricks like thrusts or sweeping blows against their opponents. Of course, the fights can be left to the AI. But then... who would not like to fight against a villain on a torch-lighted stairway?

**What characters from the legend are you including in Robin Hood?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** The splendid Will Scarlet, mighty Little John, lovely Maid Marian, the good-hearted Friar Tuck, and of course the heroic Robin Hood. Besides them there are four more playable characters that are not part of the legend, but will add some more detail and special abilities.

**Will characters have different abilities or skills? If so, could you tell us about these?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** Every character is able to wield a melee weapon. In the case of Robin Hood that will be a staff when he is in Sherwood or a sword when he infiltrates castles. And, of course, he uses his famous longbow. Aside from that the characters will have some special manoeuvres, items or skills that come in handy if the player decides to avoid fights and sneak his way to success.

**Robin Hood and his band of Merry Men were famous for hiding in Sherwood Forest in their Lincoln-green outfits. Will there be any stealth elements or missions in the game built around this idea?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** We will supply each of the characters with all the skills and items necessary so that they are able to succeed either by fighting their way through a mission or, if they like, by hiding and sneaking. It's up to the player to decide which way he wants to solve a mission. Of course, there will be missions where hiding is not the best of options and missions where you'd be a fool to try and fight – but you can try.

Take the ambush missions for example: You have to hide until the convoy has stopped by one of your traps. But you won't accomplish anything if you stay hidden in the woods. So you have to sneak into good positions to start an attack and use the traps at the crossroad to your advantage.

**Will players actually be able to indiscriminately rob from the rich whenever they desire, or will they be restricted by mission parameters?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** If the question is whether the players are able to roam the countryside and ambush every single noble in sight, the answer is no, there won't be such a feature. The starting point for every mission is a map showing all the locations of the game. There will be several missions available at any given time, both story missions and random missions. In random missions, for example, the player's goal will be to rob a taxman or weapons transport – both earning him money. In story missions not only the story will advance, but he will also be able to collect more money through chests or moneybags. After all, getting the money for King Richard's return is the main object.

Given that there will be story missions and random missions available at the same time, the player will have to make the hard choice of what to do next.

**Robin Hood looks like Desperados but in a different setting. Could you tell us about any new gameplay, graphical or sound elements that you're adding that didn't appear in the Wild West game?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** Only the basic technical elements of Desperados are left in Robin Hood. A new method of compressing graphical data gives us the possibility to double the number of animation steps for each character. Furthermore they grew in size about 25 percent compared to Desperados, which means that you'll see more detailed characters.

There are a lot of new gameplay elements in Robin Hood. For one, there will be several "amulets". You could say they are a kind of Joker – giving you, for example, one last chance if Robin is knocked unconscious and threatened to be killed. Then of course the fighting system I mentioned earlier. It adds a lot of possibilities to combat.

The new A.I. routines enable both PCs as well as NPCs to act more intelligently. Every action a NPC does is calculated with a variety of variables. Scripts are rarely used and only when necessary. An example would be: Robin and Will encounter a single guard. What happens next depends on the guard's abilities. A guard capable of handling both Will and Robin would attack right away. If the guard is standing atop the gate house for example, a script would prohibit him from doing that – it's not very realistic, that a guard would open the gates, just to fight to people outside. A more cautious guard would try to retreat in order to find help, or alarm an officer who will then gather all available men to hunt down the intruders.

**Why will Robin Hood appeal to gamers?**

Jean-Marc Haessig: It will appeal to the players because they will be able to play one of the most renowned and popular outlaws in history. And because it will be even better than Desperados. We bet that players will have very much fun with Robin Hood and his Merry Men!
