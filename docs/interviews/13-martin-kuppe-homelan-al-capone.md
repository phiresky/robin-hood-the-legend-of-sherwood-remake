After exploring the Wild West in Desperados and the Middle Ages in Robin Hood, developer Spellbound heads for another historical period with Al Capone as their upcoming tactical strategy game will bring the 1930's gangster period to life. In the first interview about the game, HomeLAN got a chance to chat with Spellbound game designer Martin Kuppe to find out more about their plans for Al Capone.

**HomeLAN** \- First, were you happy with the reaction from gamers and critics for Robin Hood?

**Martin Kuppe** \- Yes, we were. Most of the reactions and reviews were quite positive. Things that have been criticized give inspiration to improvements in our future games.

**HomeLAN** \- How did the idea come about to make a game based on American 1930's gangsters?

**Martin Kuppe** \- We wanted a universe that is quite different from the Wild West and the Middle Ages, but war and SF scenarios are not very original as they are used in many games. So we had the idea of making a game in the Prohibition time.

**HomeLAN** \- How much historical research are you doing for the game?

**Martin Kuppe** \- We have read some books about the prohibition time: A Capone biography, a book about Eliot Ness, and "The Untouchables" by Eliot Ness himself. The storyline and some missions are inspired by the history, and most names in the game will be authentic. Moreover our artists are doing a lot of historical research on style and architecture of the Prohibition time.

**HomeLAN** \- What can you tell us about the storyline for the game?

**Martin Kuppe** \- The storyline is divided into two parts, corresponding to the mafia and police campaign. In the mafia campaign, the player plays the role of Frank "The Enforcer" Nitti, the right-hand man of Al Capone. Frank Nitti and his men conquer the city of Chicago quarter by quarter, until Al Capone reigns over Chicago. During these missions, they discover a conspiracy around murder and dirty money...

The police campaign begins when Capone is the king of Chicago. The player controls Eliot Ness, a federal agent in charge of the fight against the mob. The feds free quarter after quarter from the claws of Al Capone, and discover more about the conspiracy. At the end, there is a showdown against Al Capone, and the men at the origin of the conspiracy are uncovered. You can also play the police campaign first - in this case, the mafia campaign is a review on what happened before.

**HomeLAN** \- What playable characters will you be able to use in Al Capone?

**Martin Kuppe** \- For both campaigns there will be about 15 playable characters, each of them having his own character, abilities and look. They will evolve by upgrades or by gaining experience, like in RPGs. The principal characters will be Frank Nitti for the mob and Eliot Ness for the feds.

**HomeLAN** \- What sort of locations will be seen in the game?

**Martin Kuppe** \- The majority of the missions will play inside buildings. All locations will reflect the typical feeling of the prohibition time period.

**HomeLAN** \- How would you describe the gameplay for Al Capone?

**Martin Kuppe** \- Al Capone is a team-based RTS like Robin Hood, but it provides more tactical challenge as the strength of PCs and adversaries are more balanced. You can no more confront 20 adversaries with a lonely hero and hope to survive. Moreover, you can no more detect enemies outside the view volume of your PCs. In consequence, you will proceed more cautiously, as you do not know who is behind the next corner. When sneaking around, you may profit from shadow zones to hide. But sometimes gunfights are inevitable - when you are discovered, when you attack a room full of enemies and so on. In this case you should take advantage of cover, use your firearms and grenades optimally and take care of not getting out of ammo.

There are some differences between the gameplay in the mob campaign and the cop campaign. When controlling the gangsters, you may kill everyone you want without seeing your men going to prison - as long as there stay no witnesses! You can get rid of witnesses by bribing or killing them. But take care: If you leave too much innocent dead bodies behind you, the popularity of the mafia will drop and you get less new men. On the other side, the cops must be much more cautious with their guns:
When a cop kills a gangster without being attacked, he can loose his police mark. He has even not the right of arresting a gangster if he has no evidence against him. To get evidence, you must first interrogate witnesses and examine objects.

So, a typical mafia mission consists of the three phases "Approach - Strike - Take care of witnesses". A typical cop mission goes the other way round: "Take care of witnesses - Approach - Arrest".

**HomeLAN** \- What weapons will be used in the game?

**Martin Kuppe** \- There will be different types of firearms: Colt .38, Winchester, pump gun and the Thomson sub-machine gun, better known as "Tommygun". Then there will be multiple close combat weapons: Switchblade knife, baseball bat, knuckleduster and the medic's secret weapon: Chloroform. Furthermore you can use throwing knifes and pineapple grenades. Besides the weapons, there are other objects to interact with NPCs and PCs: Ropes or handcuffs to tie up adversaries, medipacks to heal your comrades, money to bribe witnesses, and so on.

Note that now, we have real inventories and every PC can in principle use every weapon. His capacity in using this weapon depends on his abilities.

**HomeLAN** \- What other unique gameplay elements will be used in Al Capone?

**Martin Kuppe** \- In critical situations, you can switch to the Slow Motion Mode: The game runs six times slower, giving you the time to react faster than your enemies. During slow motion mode, there are some nice motion blur effects, the music changes, and the sounds are adapted. You will even see bullets flying through the air.

**HomeLAN** \- What can you tell us about the graphics engine in the game?

**Martin Kuppe** \- The engine is an evolution of the Robin Hood engine: A 2D engine with 3D data to handle projectile trajectories, sight obstacles and so on. We have made some improvements to the engine: First of all the engine now allows recolouring the sprites "on the fly". This means that two humans can use the same sprite but in different colours of jacket, hat, shoes etc. In consequence every one of the 30 PCs will have his own unique look, although we can technically limit to 8 different sprites for the playable characters.

Based on this system the engine supports now also an adaptive illumination on sprites. In a dark corner the sprite will be much darker as besides a bright lamp - as long as the lamp has not been destroyed by a bullet or a well-placed hit with the baseball bat.

**HomeLAN** \- Will there be any multiplayer modes in Al Capone?

**Martin Kuppe** \- No. The gameplay is not adapted to make a multiplayer game with.

**HomeLAN** \- What is the current status of the game and when will it be released to stores?

**Martin Kuppe** \- We have finished the game design and started production. The game will be in stores before Christmas 2003

**HomeLAN** \- A while back it was announced that Spellbound would be working on a game called Vampire Hunter. What is the current status on that game?

**Martin Kuppe** \- The Vampire Hunter project has been buried, but we did not yet put a stake through its heart. Perhaps it will return to life some day… ;o)

**HomeLAN** \- Finally, is there anything else you wish to say concerning Al Capone?

**Martin Kuppe** \- I think Al Capone will please many kinds of gamers: Not only players who like infiltration games, but also fans of tactical games like Jagged Alliance 2. There will be three degrees of difficulty, and in easy mode also occasional gamers will enjoy the game.
