## robinhood-game.de — Daniel Würstl

- Interview subject: Daniel Würstl
- Original URL: http://robinhood-game.de:80/stuff/interviews/daniel_wuerstl.php
- Wayback capture: https://web.archive.org/web/20040605114553/http://robinhood-game.de:80/stuff/interviews/daniel_wuerstl.php
- Preserved source: [`daniel_wuerstl.php`](sources/daniel_wuerstl.php)
- Language: preserved as published; no translation applied

**robinhood-game.de:** _Der obligatorische Anfang: Kannst du uns zuerst eine kleine Einleitung zu deiner Person geben?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Jung, gerade noch ledig und direkt nach dem Studium der BWL mit Schwerpunkt "Marketing - Kommunikation" bei Spellbound gelandet.
Meine Computerspielkarriere ist jedoch viel beeindruckender: Beginnend mit Pong auf VC20 und Lunar Lander auf C64 mit Datasette ist sie bis heute ungebrochen. Über 20 Jahre Spielerfahrung :-)

**robinhood-game.de:** _Wie ist der momentane Status von "Chicago 1930"? Wann ist der Release-Termin, ist schon eine Demo festgesetzt und wie sieht es mit einer offiziellen Seite aus?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Einen festen Temrin gibt es noch nicht. Rechtzeitig zu Weihnachten ist es aber da. Die PC Games war neulich da und wird einen Bericht mit Videos über Chicago bringen.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Warum habt ihr euch entschieden, das "Vampire Hunter" Projekt vorerst sein zu lassen und doch lieber "Chicago 1930" zu entwickeln?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Hm, da muss du Armin fragen. Es waren wohl beides gute Ideen und man musste sich einfach für eine entscheiden.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Wie sieht es mit der Grafik aus? Bietet die aufgebohrte Version der bereits aufgebohrten Engine noch genug Möglichkeiten um mit aktuellen Titeln mitzuhalten?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Das ist meines Erachtens vor allem Geschmacksache. Wer auf gorbpixelige 3D Geschichten wegen der Dynamik steht ist mit Chicago natürlich weniger glücklich als jemand, der das Detail und die augenschmeichelnden Grafiken sucht. Die gibt es in Chicago und ihr werdet überrascht sein, wie abwechslungsreich Räume in den 30er eingerichtet waren :-)

**robinhood-game.de:** _Martin Kuppe hat mal gesagt, dass die Mehrheit der Missionen innerhalb von Gebäuden spielen wird. Wie müssen wir uns das vorstellen? Ist das ganze Level dann ein einziges großes Gebäude, oder ist es ein Ausschnitt aus einem Stadtviertel, das überwiegend aus großen, begehbaren Häusern besteht? Warum habt ihr euch entschlossen, mehr Gebäude zu integrieren?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Meistens ist das Level ein großes Gebäude. Nur weil das Level grundsätzlich im Gebäude ist, heißt das übrigens nicht, das man nicht von der Umgebung sieht: durch die Ansicht ist meistens auch die Außenmauer sichtbar und immer ist es möglich, durch offene Fenster an den Fenstersimsen entlangzuklettern... Die Gebäude bringen viel Abwechsung in das Spiel: immer nur dunkle Hinterhöfe wäre ja langweilig. So gibt es Motels und Hotels, einen spektakulären Hauptbahnhof, Bordelle uvm.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Zur Story: Kannst du uns schon ein paar mehr Details über die Story verraten als "Die Mafia muss die Stadt erobern, die Cops müssen sie wieder zurückerobern"?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Nein sorry, dazu müsst ihr es selber spielen. Oder möchtet ihr immer schon vorher das Ende der Geschichte erfahren?

**robinhood-game.de:** _Wird es wieder einen linearen oder einen nicht-linearen Spielverlauf geben? Gibt es infolge dessen wieder mehr oder weniger Videos zwischen den Missionen?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Meistens bietet sich die Auswahl verschiedener Missionen an, was von strategischer Bedeutung ist. Es macht beispielsweise einen Unterschied, ob ich zuerst das Viertel mit dem Trainingscenter oder das mit dem Waffenlader erobere, weil das eben auch die entsprechenden Ressourcen eröffnet. Die Missionen bauen jedoch nicht direkt aufeinander auf.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Es heißt ja, dass der Spieler in "Chicago 1930", ähnlich wie in "Robin Hood", wieder Gegenstände einsammeln und seine Charaktere trainieren kann. Gibt es deshalb wieder eine Art "Hauptquartier" oder habt ihr euch was völlig neues einfallen lassen?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Ja, es gibt sozusagen ein "Stammviertel", vom dem aus die beiden kontrahierenden Parteien beginnen und letztendlich ist es das Ziel, das des Gegners zu erreichen und einzunehmen. Die Charaktere entwickeln sich im Laufe der Zeit und werden so zu einem Team aus Spezialisten, das wohlüberlegt eingesetzt werden will.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Wie ist das mit den ungefähr 30 spielbaren Charakteren? Sind diese mehr in der Art der Merry Men aus "Robin Hood" oder sind das alles Protagonisten? Warum verwendet ihr so viele Leute?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Nun es gibt auch hier einen Hauptprotagonisten, der in jedem Level mitspielt. Das restliche Team lässt sich dann aber je nach Anforderung zusammenstellen. Da es einige verschiedene Eigenschaftswerte für die Charaktere gibt, ist es sinnvoll aus mehreren wählen zu können, um einen mit einer optimalen Eigenschaftenkombination verfügbar zu haben.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Im Interview bei HomeLan heißt es, dass ihr für jeden spielbaren Charakter ein richtiges Inventar haben werdet und dass jeder so ziemlich jede Waffe nutzen kann. Trotzdem soll es ein Taktikspiel, wie wir es kennen, werden. Was tut ihr, damit "Chicago 1930" nicht zu einem schlechtem Mix aus "Jagged Alliance" und "Desperados"/"Commandos" wird?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Wir machen einen guten Mix daraus :-)

**robinhood-game.de:** _Und nun zu Desperados 2: Es heißt, ihr arbeitet schon seit Anfang dieses Jahres an "Desperados 2: Coopers Revenge" und der vorraussichtliche Release-Termin sei Ende 2004. Stimmen diese Daten? Wie es der momentane Entwicklungsstatus?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Ende 2004 ist optimistisch gerechnet. Da es ja komplett in 3D sein wird und mit dem Vorgänger hohe Erwartungen erfüllen muss, kann es auch etwas länger dauern. Im Moment wird an der Demo für Präsentationen bei Publishern gearbeitet. Es gibt bereits einen "spielbaren" Level mit Gegnern und Ziel...

**robinhood-game.de:** _Es gab (und gibt immernoch) sehr viel Aufregung und Verwirrung wegen der Ankündigung von "Desperados 2". Vorallem, weil Armin Gessert bereits einmal gesagt hat: "Ein Desperados 2 wird es von Spellbound definitiv nicht geben". Warum habt ihr euch dennoch dazu entschlossen, das Spiel zu entwickeln? Hat Infogrames (bzw. Atari) nun doch endlich nachgegeben?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Ach das sind alles lange und unspannende Geschichten... Wir sollten uns vielleicht einfach darüber freuen, dass man sich nun dazu entschieden hat, was?

**robinhood-game.de:** _Habt ihr schon einen Publisher für das Spiel oder seit ihr vielleicht gerade mitten in den Verhandlungen? Wenn ja, welches Logo, außer dem von Spellbound, werden wir auf der Packung sehen?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Der Publisher will erst noch bestimmt werden...

**robinhood-game.de:** _Die meisten Desperados bzw. Robin Hood Fans stehen einer 3D-Grafik nicht gerade freundlich gegenüber. Weshalb seit ihr von der altbewährten Methode "2D-Grafik über einem 3D-Gerüst" abgekommen?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Vor allem deshalb, weil unsere Programmierer keinen Bock mehr auf die alte Engine haben und etwas neues machen wollen. Es sind aber noch immer die gleichen Leute mit dem gleichen Ehrgeiz, ein richtig gutes Spiel zu machen, am Werk. Ihr könnt also unbesorgt sein und darauf vertrauen, dass es neben hässlichen 3D Spielen in Zukunft auch sehr ansehnliche geben wird.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Die Desperados-Fanwelt ist zur Zeit ziemlich gespalten. Die einen wollen richtig große, aber nur gute, Veränderungen, die anderen haben Angst, dass nur alles "verschlimmbessert" wird und wollen alles beim alten belassen. Wie sieht es nun wirklich aus? Habt ihr wirklich einschneidende Veränderungen geplant oder nur kleine Erweiterungen? Wie sehen diese Veränderungen/Erweiterungen in etwa aus?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Das ist im Detail noch zu früh zu sagen. Es gibt ja noch nicht einmal eine ausgearbeitete Storry. Das wichtigste, die Stimmung im Spiel, ist aber schon im ersten Demolevel zu spüren und ich bin sehr zuversichtlich, das auch D2 eine sehr dichte atmosphärische haben wird, die den Fans von D1 gefallen wird.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Wie steht es um das Gerücht, Desperados 2 könnte aus der Ego-Perspektive gespielt werden. Ist da was dran? Wenn ja, warum, wieso, weshalb?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Hier wird es eine variable Ansicht geben, die die Vorteile der Ego mit den Vorteilen einer Betrachterperspektive vereint.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Wie steht es um die Charaktere? Gibt es neue, gibt es andere, gibt es vielleicht sogar weniger? Werden die Charaktere neue Fähigkeiten haben?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Tja, die alten und wahrscheinlich auch ein paar neue.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Ein enorm wichtiges Thema: Werden für die Charaktere die gleichen Synchronsprecher wie in Teil 1 engagiert?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Da bin ich überfragt. Wüsste aber nicht, was dem entgegensteht.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Kannst du uns schon ein paar erste Details oder zumindest die grobe Richtung der Story verraten? Mich persönlich würde es sehr interessieren, warum es ausgerechnet "Coopers Revenge" heißt. Cooper will sich doch nicht etwa für das ganze Hin und Her mit den Webisodes und dem AddOn rächen, oder etwa doch?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
:-)
Coopers Revenge ist bisher mehr ein Arbeitstitel. Viele sind damit nicht glücklich, eben weil es nicht wirklich einen Grund zur Rache gibt...

**robinhood-game.de:** _Habt ihr schon einen Mann, der die Musik (in "Chicago 1930" und in "Desperados 2") machen wird? [Anmerkung: Ich meine damit, ob Tapio Muley auch wieder mit von der Partie sein wird]_

**Daniel Würstl:**
sieht verdammt so aus

**robinhood-game.de:** _Jetzt, da es offiziell ein Desperados 2 geben wird, wie steht ihr nun dem Thema Webisodes bzw. AddOn (für Teil 1) gegenüber. Gibt es jetzt wieder eine Chance, nun doch endlich die neuen Levels spielen zu können? Und darüber hinaus: So ziemlich alle Fans wünschen sich mal ein KLARES Statement in Bezug auf den Editor bzw. die Möglichkeit bzw. die Erlaubnis um ein paar Mods für Desperados zu erstellen. Seit mehr als einem Jahr, versuchen wir, mal mehr mal weniger intensiv, euch dieses Statement bzw. gleich den ganzen Editor zu entlocken. Wenigstens das Statement könnte doch jetzt fällig sein._

**Daniel Würstl:**
Das gleiche habe ich erst mich dann, als das ergebnislos blieb, auch Armin gefragt: hier seine Originalantwort:

Tja, wenn Du die Rechte mit Infogrames klärst und zudem ein paar Monate das Teil so umprogrammierst, dass auch ein Normalsterblicher damit umgehen kann, wenn Du die Dokumentation dazu schreibst, und wenn Du dann die Support-Anfragen bearbeitest, können wir den Leveleditor gerne rausgeben.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Wie war die Games Convention? Hat's dir gefallen? Hat sich der eigene Stand gelohnt oder plant ihr eventuell schon für's nächste Jahr?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Ja, die ersten zwei Tage waren noch recht spannend. Dann wurde es langsam anstrengend. Aber wir waren schließlich dort, um zu arbeiten. Und das haben wir ja auch getan. 9000 Postkarten und 5000 Poster wurden verteilt und unser Stand sah wirklich seeeehr cool aus. Daher denke ich schon, dass sich die Messe irgendwann gelohnt haben wird, auch wenn so was natürlich wirtschaftlich nicht zu ermessen ist.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Möchtest du abschließend vielleicht noch irgendetwas ergänzen oder etwas, bis jetzt noch unerwähntes, hinzufügen?_

**Daniel Würstl:**
Da möchte ich nur noch einen netten Sinnspruch eines Kollegen zu eurer Erheiterung springen lassen:

...just because you're paranoid doesn't mean we don't follow you...

_Wir bedanken uns für das aufschlussreiche Interview. Ich denke, dass die gesamte Community jetzt schlauer als vorher ist (das auf jeden Fall, aber mir persönlich hat es wirklich gefallen und es sind einige richtig interessante Infos dabei!).
Danke Daniel Würstl!_

## robinhood-game.de — Tapio Muley

- Interview subject: Tapio Muley
- Original URL: http://www.robinhood-game.de:80/stuff/interviews/tapio_muley.php
- Wayback capture: https://web.archive.org/web/20040530020321/http://www.robinhood-game.de:80/stuff/interviews/tapio_muley.php

**robinhood-game.de:** _Könntest du Dich zu Beginn vielleicht ein bisschen vorstellen damit wir einen kleinen Eindruck von Dir erhalten? Wo liegen Deine Interessen? Was machst Du beruflich?_

**Tapio Muley:**
Mein Name ist Tapio Muley und man kennt mich im Internet unter dem Pseudonym "Shao-Lee". Ich bin 32 Jahre alt und komponiere schon seit über 13 Jahren Computer-Musik. Die elektronische Musik, insbesondere aus den 80er Jahren, hat mich schon sehr früh geprägt und lässt mich auch bis heute nicht mehr los. Durch meine (bis heute anhaltende) Begeisterung für Computerspiele war es für mich schon immer ein Traum gewesen, einmal den kompletten Soundtrack für solch ein Computerspiel zu schreiben.

Mein tägliches Brot verdiene ich als Netzwerkadministrator. Hier betreue ich ein EDV-Netzwerk mit ca. 40 Arbeitsplätzen.

Die Musik selbst ist ein intensives Hobby von mir, wobei der Ausdruck "Hobby" mittlerweile auch nicht mehr so ganz zutreffens ist ;) Aber meine Einstellung hat sich nicht verändert: Musik muss Spass machen - nicht nur für den Komponisten, sondern insbesondere auch für den Zuhörer !

**robinhood-game.de:** _Arbeitest Du für irgendeine Firma oder bist du freischaffender Künstler?_

**Tapio Muley:**
Ich bin freiberuflich tätig, arbeite also selbstständig und unabhängig.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Hast Du schon des öfteren für PC-Spiele komponiert und in welcher Weise warst Du an der Entstehung von Spellbound's Robin Hood beteiligt?_

**Tapio Muley:**
Musik für die Computerszene selbst mache ich schon seit jeher. Robin Hood war für mich jedoch angesichts des Umfanges und der Tatsache, dass dieses Spiel auch tatsächlich einmal den Weg in die Ladenregale finden wird, eine neue Herausforderung ! Meine Aufgaben bei Spellbound waren die Komposition der IN-Game-Musik sowie Arbeiten im Bereich der Soundeffekte von Robin Hood.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Kannst Du uns etwas über die Zusammenarbeit mit der Firma Spellbound erzählen? Wie können wir uns die Zusammenarbeit vorstellen - wird alles per E-Mail abgewickelt oder warst Du auch vor Ort ? Wie bist Du eigentlich zur Firma Spellbound gekommen?_

**Tapio Muley:**
Man kann nur einen gewissen Teil über eMail abwickeln - beispielsweise Soundupdates verschicken oder einen kleineren Gedankenaustausch vollziehen. Gemeinsame Besprechungen sind insbesondere im Entwicklungsprozess unabdingbare Voraussetzungen für ein gutes Gesamtergebnis. Aus diesem Grund war ich auch selbst sehr oft bei der Firma Spellbound und wir haben uns dann gemeinsam über meine Ausarbeitungen unterhalten.
An dieser Stelle möchte ich ausdrücklich die Zusammenarbeit mit der Firma Spellbound loben ! Für einen Musiker ist die Information zu einem Computerspiel das "A und O" ! All meine Fragen fanden immer ein offenes Ohr und das gemeinsame Ziel, Robin Hood, stand stets im Vordergrund.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Hattest Du für jedes Stück eine feste Vorstellung oder hast Du während des Komponierens eher spontan gehandelt? Du hast ja schon früher erwähnt, dass Dir die Stimmung der einzelnen Musikstücke sehr am Herzen liegt. Wo hast Du Dir Deine Inspirationen geholt und woher wusstest du dass die Musik dann auch zum entsprechenden Level passt?_

**Tapio Muley:**
Wie bereits oben schon erwähnt, ist die Information zu einem Spiel das "A und O" für einen Komponisten. Man macht sich also zuerst grundsätzlich einmal Gedanken zum Gesamtthema, sprich: sollte die Musik zu "Robin Hood" eher Elemente aus der Filmmusik enthalten oder doch eher mittelalterliche Schwerpunkte setzen. Diese Frage haben wir gemeinsam (Spellbound und ich) sehr intensiv erörtert, da ja letztendlich hierin das Gesamtkonzept verborgen liegt. Wir haben uns für einen "Mittelweg" entschieden, welcher Elemente aus verschiedenen Musikgenres vereint. Wichtig war es, die Atmosphäre (Stimmung) von Robin Hood auch durch die Musik zu unterstreichen. Das lag mir persönlich auch sehr am Herzen.

Wenn das Gesamtkonzept steht, geht es an die Ausarbeitung der einzelnen Level-musiken. Um meine Musik den Levels anzupassen, haben mir die tollen Umgebungsgrafiken als Leitfaden gedient. Das müsst Ihr Euch so vorstellen:
Sämtliche Levels habe ich mir auf Din-A3 ausgedruckt. Die Bilder habe ich dann neben meinem Keyboard aufgestellt und mich dann von ihnen inspirieren lassen. Mein Ziel war es, die Stimmungen, die diese wunderschönen Grafiken vermitteln, auch in der Musik umzusetzen, um das Spielgefühl zu intensivieren. Jedem Level konnte ich somit auch musikalisch eine persönliche Note verleihen.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Hast Du alle Deine Ideen umsetzen können und hast Du Deiner Meinung nach Dein Ziel erreicht?_

**Tapio Muley:**
Alle meine Ideen habe ich leider nicht umsetzen können. Das hatte verschiedene Gründe: zum einen waren die zeitlichen Terminsetzungen sehr eng, zum anderen gabs auch die eine oder andere technische Hürde zu nehmen. Hinter dem Ergebnis stehe ich aber trotzdem voll und ganz und bin auch ein bischen stolz darauf, dass ich das (auch mir persönlich) definierte Ziel erreichen konnte :-)

**robinhood-game.de:** _Die Musikstücke, die wir bis jetzt gehört haben, klingen sehr professionell. Mit welchen Hilfsmitteln arbeitest Du?_

**Tapio Muley:**
Dankeschön !

Grundsätzlich wird die Musik elektronisch erzeugt, d.h. mit elektronischen Klangerzeugern sowie einem PC. In meinem Fall sind das Synthesizer (elektronische Tasteninstrumente), ein Musikcomputer (PC), sowie Hardware für das Abmischen der Sounds sowie für die Aufnahme. Das Herzstück meiner Anlage stellt mit Sicherheit meine Workstation "Triton" von Korg dar, was ein äußerst inspirierender Synthesizer ist.

**robinhood-game.de:** _Gibt es noch weitere Stücke von Dir im Internet (oder sogar im Handel) und wo könnten wir diese bekommen?_

**Tapio Muley:**
Es gibt in der Tat weitere Musikstücke von mir, aber leider nicht im Handel. Vielleicht ergibt sich aber einmal die Gelegenheit, auch für den "Stand-Alone"-Musikmarkt zu produzieren.
Ich arbeite derzeit mit Hochdruck an der Fertigstellung meiner privaten Internetseite. In den nächsten Tagen ist es soweit, dann geht meine neue Webside "www.shao-lee.de" online. Dort werdet Ihr neben zahlreichen Informationen auch einige Soundtracks zum Download finden. Habt also noch ein bischen Geduld ;)

**robinhood-game.de:** _Es gibt ja sehr viele junge Komponisten, die ebenfalls in dieses Buisness einsteigen wollen, um als Sounddesigner für professionelle Spiele zu arbeiten. Welche Vorraussetzungen müssen sie erfüllen und was kannst Du ihnen als Tipp mit auf den Weg geben?_

**Tapio Muley:**
Ganz wichtige Faktoren sind hier Eigeninitiative und Kreativität !

Es gibt tatsächlich sehr viele junge Künster, die versuchen, in dieser Branche Fuß zu fassen und die auch professionell einsteigen wollen.

Die Musikuntermalung in Computerspielen hat sich in den letzten Jahren jedoch sehr verändert. Waren es früher eher "Modul"-basierende Soundtracks, die aufwendig programmiert werden mussten, so gleichen heutige Kompositionsarbeiten eher den professionellen Musikproduktionen in der Film- und Fernsehindustrie. Um diesen Qualitätsstandard zu erreichen, sind nicht unerhebliche Investitionen erforderlich, da gutes Equipment sehr teuer ist. Bereits diese Hürde macht es für Einsteiger sehr schwierig.

Ein unersetzbarer Faktor ist aber auch die Kreativität eines Musikers ! Um sein Können unter Beweis zu stellen, bieten sich beispielsweise Freewareprojekte an, die man im Internet zu Hauf findet. Das können MOD's (Modifications) oder auch kleinere Spiele sein. Wenn man dann auch mal den einen oder anderen Soundtrack auf diese Weise veröffentlicht hat, hat der Künstler nicht nur eine Refferenz, sondern auch die Chance, dass auf seine Musik auch professionelle Produktionsfirmen aufmerksam werden.
Auch schadet es nie, einmal eine Demo-CD an hiesige Spieleschmieden zu senden. Solche Einsendungen landen mit Sicherheit nie ungehört im Mülleinmer. Wie bereits oben erwähnt: die Eigeninitiative ist hier auch ganz wichtig !

Bitte berücksichtigt, dass dies meine persönlich gemachten Erfahrungen sind !

**robinhood-game.de:** _Brennt Dir zum Schluss noch irgendwas auf der Zunge, dass Du unbedingt noch loswerden möchtest? Oder hast Du vielleicht eine kleine Schlussbemerkung?_

**Tapio Muley:**
Ich glaube, man kann sich kaum vorstellen, wieviel Arbeit und Kreativität in solch einem grossen Spieleprojekt steckt, wenn man den Entwicklungsprozess nicht selbst miterlebt hat.

Für uns alle wünsche ich mir, dass Robin Hood die Erwartungen der Fans erfüllen wird und das Spiel den Erfolg erhält, den es dann auch verdient hat.

_Wir bedanken uns für das aufschlussreiche Interview. Ich denke, dass die gesamte Robin Hood Community jetzt einen kleinen Eindruck gewonnen hat, wie die Musik entstanden ist und wie Spellbound und ihre Mitarbeiter sich untereinander austauschen.
Danke Tapio Muley!_

## robinhood-game.de — Mischa Strecker

- Interview subject: Mischa Strecker
- Original URL: http://www.robinhood-game.de:80/stuff/interviews/mischa_strecker.php

_Die meisten werden die Antwort auf diese Frage bereits kennen, aber für die die es noch nicht wissen: Was ist deine Aufgabe bei Spellbound?_

Meine Hauptarbeit besteht in der Pressearbeit für Spellbound Entertainment. Ich verfasse Pressemitteilungen und halte den Kontakt zur Presse und Agenturen. Da diese Arbeit allein meinen Tag - noch - nicht vollständig ausfüllt, kümmere ich mich um die Community von Spellbound und schreibe und redigiere Handbücher, Dialogtexte und so weiter.

_Du hast ja Spellbound Forum geschrieben, dass ihr den Gedanken von Robin Hood schon sehr lange habt. Spukte jetzt die Desperados oder die Robin Hood Idee länger in euren Köpfen herum?_

Ganz klar Desperados. Die Idee zu Desperados kam, als Armin und Jean-Marc einen Western im Fernsehen sahen. Das ist inzwischen über 3 Jahre her. Die Idee zu Robin Hood wurde erst im Spätsommer 2001 "geboren".

_Es ging ja einmal das Gerücht um, Desperados sei nur dazu da gewesen, um Programmiererfahrungen für Robin Hood zu sammeln. Ist da was dran?_

Nein. Desperados war mehr als "nur" ein "Lernspiel" für unsere Programmierer. Darin steckt die volle Arbeitskraft eines ambitionierten Teams, das eines der besten Spiele für den PC erschaffen wollte - und damit Erfolg hatte. Natürlich sammelte das Spellbound-Team während der Entwicklung von Desperados Erfahrungen und setzt diese nun in Robin Hood um. Es wäre doch traurig, wenn Spellbound auf der Stelle treten würde und ständig dieselben Spiele ohne Neuerungen produzieren würde. Nicht?

_Die Grafik-Engine von Robin Hood wird ja eine erweiterte Form der Desperados-Engine sein. Was habt ihr alles an ihr verändert?_

Ein besseres Komprimierungsverfahren erlaubt es uns, die Bilddaten um das vierfache zu vergrößern. Dadurch konnten wir zum Beispiel die Animationsphasen der Charaktere verdoppeln und die Charaktere im Spiel etwa 25% größer gestalten. Das Ergebnis sind flüssigere Bewegungen und detailreichere Figuren.

_Böse Zungen behaupten ja, dass die KI bei Desperados an manchen Stellen versagt hat. Habt ihr euch nun, speziell wegen dieser Aussage, besonders viel Mühe gegeben bei der KI von Robin Hood?_

Auch eine natürliche Intelligenz versagt an einigen Stellen ;-). Die KI von Robin Hood ist fast von Grund auf neu geschrieben worden. Das war nötig um den NPCs neue Fähigkeiten beizubringen und den Kampf mit mittelalterlichen Waffen darstellen zu können. Die Schergen von Prinz John bewegen sich anders als die Gegner in Desperados. Sie teilen sich in defensive oder offensive Formationen auf und hören auf Befehle, die ihnen die Offiziere erteilen. Die Soldaten sind inzwischen sogar so intelligent, dass sie auch selbst entscheiden können, ob sie in einen Kampf eingreifen sollten - oder besser abwarten, um einem gepanzerten Ritter nicht ins Handwerk zu pfuschen.

_In Robin Hood wird man ja den Schwierigkeitsgrad frei wählen können. Kann man vor jeder Mission neu wählen oder muss man sich am Anfang des Spieles entscheiden?_

Vor und während des Spiels. Vor dem Spiel indem ihr eine der drei Einstellungen wählt. Während des Spiels könnt ihr euch bestimmte Missionen durch so genannte Vorbereitungsmissionen erheblich erleichtern. Wie das genau aussieht, soll eine Überraschung werden...

Es wird drei Schwierigkeitsgrade geben, wobei "normal" in etwa dem Schwierigkeitsgrad von Desperados entspricht. Unterscheiden werden sie sich hauptsächlich durch Veränderungen an den Charakter- Eigenschaften der NPCs.

Schwerter und Schilde, Äxte, Kampfstäbe, Bögen, Dolche, Morgensterne, Keulen, Speere, Hellebarden, Äpfel und Wespennester. Robin Hood wird mit Bogen, Stab und Schwert kämpfen ... und mehr wird (noch) nicht verraten ;-)

_Auf den Screenshots ist in der linken oberen Ecke das britische Pfund-Zeichen zu sehen. Wird es in Robin Hood Geld geben und wie wird man es einsetzen können?_

Eines der wichtigsten Ziele Robin Hoods war es, Richard Löwenherz aus der Gefangenschaft freizukaufen, damit dieser wieder seinen rechtmäßigen Thron in England besteigen konnte. Diese Aufgabe haben wir auch im Spiel übernommen.

_Ebenfalls sieht man auf den Screenshots bei den Bildern der Charaktern die Bezeichnung "Merry Man B". Was hat das zu bedeuten?_

Das ist einer von drei zusätzlichen, spielbaren Charakteren, die neben den Helden wie Robin Hood, Lady Marian, Bruder Tuck, Little John, etc. im Spiel auftauchen. Es wird insgesamt neun spielbare verschiedene Charaktere mit unterschiedlichen Fähigkeiten geben.

Nein, das haben wir nicht vor. Ihr könnt aber selbst entscheiden, welcher der neun Charaktere euch in der Mission unterstützt. Natürlich wird euch das Spiel eine Gruppenzusammenstellung vorschlagen - befolgen müsst Ihr sie aber nicht.

In October of 1919, the American federal government enacted the Volstead Act, a piece of legislation providing for enforcement of the Eighteenth Amendment, which banned "the manufacture, sale, or distribution of intoxicating liquors" - defined as beverages containing more than a heady 0.5 percent alcohol. Also known as the National Prohibition Act, it went into effect in July of the following year, giving authorities the right to prosecute violators. As foolish as all this might seem now, it was characterized by Herbert Hoover as a "noble experiment". The intent was to improve the population's health and reduce crime, which would also cut the cost of running the prison system. Of course, the lawmakers completely failed to take into account simple human nature, in particular the natural resistance people have to legal meddling in the ordinary affairs of their daily lives.

The prohibition era that followed serves as the inspiration for Chicago 1930, which is currently in progress at [Spellbound Studios](http://www.spellbound.de). Described as combining strategy, stealth and action elements, the game reportedly begins with the Mafia controlling large parts of the city. A special police task force has been formed in order to break its hold. At this point, the player's role and objective may seem pretty obvious, but the developers have thrown in a twist by allowing you to play either side. The gangster forces are charged with committing criminal acts such as murders and kidnappings, while the agents of the law must take back the community quarter by quarter by accomplishing missions like investigations. To learn more, we took Lead Game Designer and AI Programmer Martin Kuppe hostage for this Chicago 1930 Interview, Part 1.

* * *

**Action Vault: How would you summarize Chicago 1930? What's it about, what type of game is it, and what kind of gameplay should players expect? And what led to the choice of the location and time?

Martin Kuppe:** Chicago 1930 is a real-time strategy game with infiltration tactics and combat action, just like Desperados or Robin Hood - I repeated this sentence 256755874 times at our stand at the E3, so I know it by heart. :o) The player has the choice between playing the good guys (FBI) or the bad guys (Mafia), with quite different gameplay for both sides.

We have chosen a Mafia setting because it is an interesting universe that has not yet used very often in team-based tactical games. Why we have chosen Chicago? Well, you know, we hesitated between Chicago and Honolulu, but we finally realized that in Honolulu, they never had many problems with the Mafia... Why 1930? Because in 1840, the streets of Chicago were quite peaceful. ;o) No, seriously, the Windy City during the prohibition is the ideal setting for a Mafia game.

**Action Vault: What more are you willing to say about the setting, the campaigns for the opposing sides, the differences between them, and the extent to which they are related to each other?

Martin Kuppe:** The game takes place in 1930 in Chicago. :o))) More exactly, the story starts in 1928 when Don Falcone arrives at Chicago. On the Mafia side, the player takes the role of Jack Beretto, the right arm of Don Falcone. The campaign consists of taking the city quarter after quarter, up to the final mission in 1930, where the objective is to kill the District Attorney in his own apartment.

The police campaign starts with the investigation of a murder. The district attorney has been murdered in his apartment... Here, the player is Edouard Nash, the chief of a group of federal agents whose task is to fight against the mob. The goal of the police campaign is to drive the Mafia out of the city and take Chicago back.

There is a storyline linking the two campaigns. In the Mafia campaign, the player learns that there is a mysterious "Baron" who secretly removes the stitches in the Falcone empire. In the police campaign, the quest continues, we find new evidence about the Baron... In the final mission, we learn who the man behind the curtain really was.

The player can also start with the police campaign and then play the Mafia campaign as a flashback. In any case he may learn the truth about the Baron before finishing the two campaigns.

**Action Vault: In addition to the police and criminal sides having separate campaigns, to what extent should we look for other elements of the gameplay to differ?

Martin Kuppe:** The game is quite different for the Mafia and for the police. Of course there are different mission goals. As a gangster, you have to kill enemy gangsters, kidnap a district attorney, destroy the beer reserves of a rival gang, assassinate a judge, etc. As a cop, you have to investigate on murders, free hostages, make police raids etc.

But it is not only about different mission goals and different suits. The gameplay is quite different. As a gangster, you can kill whoever you want - you just have to take care of the witnesses afterwards, either by killing them or by bribing them. As a cop, you don't have the right at all to commit crimes, even if nobody is looking. Above all, you are not allowed to kill people, except in cases of self-defence. When you break these rules, the guilty characters will go to jail after the mission.

Furthermore as a cop, you cannot arrest a gangster because you think he is a gangster. If you want to arrest someone, you must first follow legal steps by finding evidence and interrogating witnesses.

In general, the police gameplay is much more about infiltration, looking for evidence, interrogating witnesses, then arresting the gangsters, and shooting only when you are attacked. The Mafia gameplay is more brutal - which does not necessarily mean that you can shoot anyone and anything around with your Tommy gun from the beginning, because if you do so, you will be surrounded by a dozen cops within three seconds.

**Action Vault: On a more detailed level, what range of locations does Chicago 1930 include? How much research did you do in this regard?

Martin Kuppe:** There are eight different locations in the game, some of them consisting of two floors - a luxury hotel, a factory, a brothel, a restaurant, a train station, a harbor zone, a motel and an apartment.

Our artists did a lot of research about architecture, furniture, fashion, etc. during the prohibition era. The locations are partially inspired by both real locations and film settings - for example, the railroad station which is an allusion to the famous railroad station scene in the movie The Untouchables - including the baby carriage.

**Action Vault: As for the structure of the respective campaigns, how do they begin, and from there, do they continue in a linear progression or is there some degree of player choice?

Martin Kuppe:** You choose the next mission on the map of Chicago. Take the Mafia campaign for an example. When you start the game, you see the map of Chicago - eight quarters, corresponding to the eight locations. Five of them are colored brown, which means that they are owned by a rival gang. The remaining three are blue; they are controlled by the authorities. One of the brown quarters to the west is blinking - this is your first mission. You click on it, receive the briefing, compose your team, equip it and start the mission.

After the mission, the quarter is colored in red, which is your color, and two of the adjacent brown quarters are blinking. You can choose where to attack, and so on... This way, the player conquers the city quarter by quarter. The police campaign goes the other way round - the whole map is in red, and you have to re-paint it in blue... Thus we have two non-linear campaigns consisting of 10 missions each (eight missions to conquer the quarters and two additional missions for the storyline).

**Action Vault: Since we know the game is squad-based, how many characters does the player actually control at any one time, and when and how are its members selected?

Martin Kuppe:** As noted before, in the Mafia campaign the player character is Jack Beretto, the right-hand man of Don Falcone. In the police campaign, the player takes the role of federal agent Edouard Nash, chief of a group of untouchable agents in charge of the fight against the Mafia. The mission team always consists of the main character and up to four other characters that can be chosen freely before each mission. There is a pool of up to 15 different characters per campaign, each one having his own attribute values.

**Action Vault: What are these attributes you mentioned, and is there an advancement system that allows characters to improve in them during the course of the game?

Martin Kuppe:** There are five attributes - shooting, close combat, throwing, medicine and charisma. Furthermore, the characters may have special bonuses for some actions, for example "Tommy gun +2". The characters can train the attributes by using them - when you use, for example, grenades and throwing knives very often, your throwing attribute gets better.

You can also train your characters in the sports club once you have conquered the quarter with the "sports club" icon. This is an example of the resources placed in the Chicago map. There are the following resources: speakeasies (where you can get new men), armories (where you get access to new weapons and equipment), sports club (to train your men), tribunal (to reduce the time your characters are in jail) and newspaper (to increase popularity).

**Action Vault: How does the combat system work, what are its important features, and what is the balance between direct combat and other types of gameplay such as the obvious one, stealth?

Martin Kuppe:** The combat system is based on an intuitive point and click interface. You click on a weapon in your inventory, and then click on the adversary to use it. When it is a firearm, we will have a recoil effect on the cursor, depending on the weapon and on the character's shooting ability. Some weapons need a bit more consideration, for example, the pineapple grenade. When you have selected the grenade, you press the left mouse button down to pull the ring, and you release it to throw the grenade. It's all about timing; when you toss it too early, the adversaries will duck and take cover, or even pick up the grenade and throw it back. If you hold it longer, it may explode in your hand, which is not really a good idea. But we have some graphical help for the player. A countdown is displayed over the grenade, and you see the exact trajectory it will take.

As a general remark, you only see what your characters see. When you have seen an enemy and he leaves your 180-degree view area, he slowly fades out. When your characters hear an NPC, this is displayed as a "blip" consisting of concentric circles.

As for the balance, we have maybe 60% stealth, 40% direct combat. In general, you start a mission with stealth tactics, as they are full of adversaries. When you have cleaned up the level a bit, you can dare to use noisy weapons like guns and grenades. Often, the level ends with a big final gunfight.

**Action Vault: What kind of pace are you aiming for in the stealth and combat aspects? How will the slow-motion mode work, and will it be possible to pause the action completely to issue orders?

Martin Kuppe:** As long as you use stealth tactics, the game is relatively slow. Of course, you sometimes have to react fast; for example, when you see or hear a guard coming around the corner. When it comes to combat, it is, of course, much faster. In fact, in a big gunfight, it may become too fast to handle - therefore, we have the slow motion mode.

With a click on an icon, the game is slowed down by the factor of five. Graphically we have an "old film effect" and motion blur effects on the characters; you can see bullets flying through the air... We hear a heartbeat sound, and the in-game sounds are sampled down. In the slow motion mode, you have enough time to give orders to different characters, react instantly to enemy behavior and so on. Furthermore, you can in this mode assign targets to your characters. One will then shoot at the cop on the left until he is dead, another the cop on the right... The assigned targets are represented by dotted lines; this way, you see which characters have a target and which ones are waiting for new orders.

And, no, we have no pause mode where you give orders - if we would have a pause, nobody would use our nice slow motion mode. ;o)

And for now, of course, we thank him for what we have learned so far.

* * *

**Action Vault: During the course of the game, what assortment of weapons will be available to the player, and to what extent are they accurate in representing the actual arms of the period?

Martin Kuppe:** We have four kinds of firearms (Colt .38, Winchester, pump gun and Tommy gun), four kinds of close combat weapon (knuckleduster, baseball bat, baton and switchblade knife) and some other weapons (pineapple grenade, throwing knife, chloroform). The more weapons the player owns, the more weapons he has at his disposition. The weapon characteristics are adapted to the gameplay, not to historical facts.

**Action Vault: What were your primary objectives in designing and implementing the enemy NPCs, especially with respect to the artificial intelligence that controls their behavior?

Martin Kuppe:** The main goal was to have enemies with realistic and "human-like" behavior. Enemy NPCs react differently when they see player characters, depending on the situation. A gangster drinking his milk shake at the hotel bar will, for example, not react in the same way as a guard in front of a door, a lookout on a street corner, a cop patrolling in the street or a gangster counting money in a back room. Furthermore, the NPCs should fight in an intelligent and cooperative way - look for help, encircle the player characters, hide behind desks, choose optimal shooting position, etc.

**Action Vault: Will there be many friendly NPCs, or neutral ones like innocent bystanders? What kinds of roles will they play, and how important will they be overall?

Martin Kuppe:** The civilians play an important role in the gameplay. In the police campaign, civilian witnesses are your main source of information. In the Mafia campaign, civilians may become witnesses to your crimes. When a civilian sees one of your characters committing a murder, she becomes a witness - which is indicated by an eye symbol above her head. When you don't take care of the witness, she will tell everything to the police after the mission, and maybe your gangster will go to jail. So, better take care of the witnesses. The simplest way is to kill them - but there is a little problem. Imagine you commit a murder. There is a chambermaid who sees what you did and who runs into the restaurant. Well, you follow her and kill her in public. *bang* But eight of the 20 guests in the restaurant see you - eight more witnesses to kill... *bang, bang, bang...* Now the remaining 12 guests are also witnesses. *Bang, bang, bang, bang...* Oops, the waiter has come out of the kitchen. *Bang*. Just at this moment, five other guests come in...

In the end, you have no more surviving witnesses, and nobody will go to jail - but there is a big heap of dead witnesses in the restaurant. And a big heap of dead bodies in Chicago during the prohibition - everybody knows that this was not the Ku Klux Klan... So, if you kill all the witnesses, the popularity of the Mafia will drop, and you will get less new men. But there is another way to take care of the witnesses. Bribe them! So, if you have some bribe money in your pockets, talk with the chambermaid, give her some bills, and she has seen nothing.

**Action Vault: How many missions will there be for each side, and to what degree have you designed them to differ in terms of the gameplay and goals?

Martin Kuppe:** There will be overall 20 missions in the game, 10 for each campaign. They provide a wide variety of mission objectives. As a gangster you have, for example, to kill a judge, to destroy the beer reserves of a rival gang, to assassinate enemy gangsters, to kidnap a district attorney or to help Don Falcone out of a police raid. As a fed, you make raids, investigate murders, search for weapons in a secret weapon stock, protect a witness, free hostages, etc.

**Action Vault: It seems the game is single-player only? Is this correct, and why did you decide not to have multiplayer?

Martin Kuppe:** It's only single-player. The gameplay does not match very well with a multiplayer mode. Initially, we discussed a lot about whether a multiplayer mode is possible, but finally decided to focus on single-player.

**Action Vault: What engine are you using, and which of its features are especially important? What do you think the system requirements and recommendations for Chicago 1930 will be?

Martin Kuppe:** The engine is based on our own Desperados / Robin Hood engine, but with some improvements. First, the sprites are lighted dynamically. When a character goes into a shadow zone, he gets darker; when he approaches a light, he gets brighter. Second, we have particle effects on all kinds of broken objects - lamps, vases, plants... Furthermore, we have a sprite re-coloring system that allows having an individual sprite for each of the 30 available player characters.

As for the system requirements, the game should run on a PII 300 with 64 MB RAM and a graphics card with 8 MB.

**Action Vault: When did the idea for the game arise, and when did development begin? What is the present status, when will it be ready for release, and in which markets are you planning to release it?

Martin Kuppe:** In 2002, we discussed what kind of game we could create based on the Robin Hood engine, and finally, we had the idea that a Mafia game would be great. We started work in Q4 2002, and we are just before the alpha version. In Europe, the game will be in stores before Christmas 2003, except if an asteroid crashes down on our company. ;o)

**Action Vault: What factors will make Chicago 1930 stand out, and what kind of gamer do you see as your core target market? Approximately what duration of gameplay are you aiming for, and what level of replayability do you anticipate?

Martin Kuppe:** As far as I know, Chicago 1930 is the only infiltration tactics game where you can choose between the good guys and the bad guys. Other strong points are the RPG aspect, the background story and the slow motion mode, which transforms messy gunfights into tactical challenges.

The game will have three degrees of difficulty, so occasional players and hardcore gamers can enjoy it. The core target group is gamers who like infiltration and action games such as Commandos, Desperados and Robin Hood.
The duration of gameplay is very difficult to estimate at the current state, but I think it will be around 10 hours of gameplay. The RPG component and the non-linear campaigns will give a strong motivation to replay the game.

**Action Vault: Please tell our readers a bit about Spellbound and its history. Where is the studio located and who are the core members of the Chicago 1930 team? What experience do you have in game development?

Martin Kuppe:** Spellbound was founded in 1995 and has developed the games Perry Rhodan: Operation Eastside, Airline Tycoon, Desperados and Robin Hood. The studio is located at Kehl, a small German town just at the French border. We just have to cross the Rhine bridge, and we are in Strassbourg. Most of the team members are French.

The key members of our development team are project manager Jean-Michel Stenger, technical director Stéphane Becker, lead programmer Arno Wienholtz, lead level designer Mickael Parisot, and last but not least art director Jean-Marc Haessig - all French people. Oh, and also me. Personally, I am German, but being surrounded by French people, I speak French all day long... During last week's E3, Wanadoo reveiled their newest tactical game Chicago 1930. The game is scheduled to ship to retail shelves in Q4 2003 and in the meantime we have some new screens and a pre-packaged Q&A; with Spellbound's Armin Gessert. Read more to check out the goods ...

**Q. How did the idea of CHICAGO 1930 game concept came up?**

_AG. Tactical games perfectly match with Wanadoo's publishing policy, which provides games mixing renowned gameplays and large audience themes. Consequently, before the release of Robin Wood, the Wanadoo publishing team thought about various new tactical and strategy environments. Among them, the mafia scheme during the Prohibition got the full agreement of all of them. More than the well-knowned universe, it is appealing since it can provide a very action based game with fire weapons, and in a city environment. And this is new compared to Robin Wood. Furthermore, they immediately designed a gameplay which reinforced the stealth features. Naturally, they asked the highly creative team at Spellbound to develop the game._

**Q. Can you give us more details on the developers of CHICAGO 1930?**

_AG. Spellbound Studios. Spellbound Entertainment AG and Spellbound Studios GmbH create innovative and highly acclaimed computer games. In the same genre they have created "Desperados – Wanted Dead or Alive" and "Robin Hood – The Legend of Sherwood". They therefore have much experience in games combining tactical infiltration and combat action. The graphics in their games and the AI have earned a lot of appreciation in the press and by gamers._

**Q. Have you used any factual info and done any research?**

_AG. The game is based on the historical events in Chicago between 1921 and 1931. In the mafia campaign, the player takes the role of the right arm of a Mafioso. His goal will be to take control of the city, eliminate his opponents and make his boss the king of Chicago. In the police campaign the roles are reversed as the player is a federal agent who must free the city quarter by quarter from the iron fist of the Mafioso almighty. To add depth and credibility to the game, we have studied several books about this era: Schoenberg's Capone biography, the Eliot Ness biography by Paul W. Heimel, and last but not least the book "The Untouchables" by Eliot Ness himself. Furthermore, our artists are doing a lot of historical research concerning architecture, fashion and so on._

**Q. What is the player required to do?**

_AG. The player can compose his mission teams from 17 hoods in the mob campaign or 16 federal agents in the police campaign. In both campaigns, the player conquers quarters by playing missions. The mission goals vary from case to case. For example in the mob campaign the player must destroy a rival brewery, escort The Boss out of a brothel during a police raid, spy on a meeting of rival gangsters or eliminate a prosecutor. In the police campaign, he has to investigate a murder, arrest a group of gangsters, protect an important witness or raid a restaurant. Many missions are inspired by historical events, such as the Massacre on St. Valentine's._

**Q. In a few words, what is the idea behind this game ?**

_AG. After having the basic idea of creating a mafia game, we had about a million ideas, and the design work was (as always) to make clever choices. We decided to focus on the ideas that contributed to the infiltration and combat action and on the differences between the gameplay in the mafia and police campaigns. It is not wholly "peopling based" or "weapon inspired", but more of a combination: The game is about people using weapons against other people who are sometimes drinking and gambling._

**Q. Are resources a big factor in the game? What resources/commodities will you be able to use and acquire?**

_AG. The resources on the map of Chicago play an important role, but the gameplay focuses on the missions. The more quarters with resources that you have conquered, the more possibilities you will have in the missions: You must, for example, possess one equipment to use pump guns and switchblade knifes, two to get Winchesters, chloroform and throwing knifes, and three to have access to pineapple grenades and the Thomson sub-machinegun. Other resources provide new recruits, character upgrades, better popularity etc._

**Q. AI must play an important part in the game. How do you test the AI to ensure it works? How do you pull in the gamer?**

_AG. Like every other feature in the game, AI is constantly tested by the team during development, and also passes intensive QA tests during the debugging phase. The enemies will show different combat behaviors, depending on the situation and on their orders: open fire, hide behind a desk and fire from the cover, attack with close combat weapons, run to get more ammo, attack player characters from different sides, run to get help, run and hide, surrender, menace the player characters etc._

**Q. What are the major differences between Robin Hood-The Legend of Sherwood- and Chicago 1930 ?**

_AG. You have several differences :_

  * _You can choose your side: play the mob or the cops & manage a team of 5 characters_
  * _Characters have five different features: shooting, close combat, throwing, charisma, first aid._
  * _At the end of a mission or after a training session, the player can choose how he wants to attribute the points he won_
  * _In scenes where there is a lot of action happening at the same time, you can use the slow motion mode in order to handle the five playable characters more efficiently._
  * _Improved Artificial Intelligence. Indeed, all enemies can do all actions (shoot, throw, run away, etc). They have different types of reactions according to the situation (alone, in a group, far from their team, etc)_
  * _Non-playable characters have more realistic behaviour (for example the witness of a murder will run away and will later recognize the killer, etc)_
  * _A dynamic lighting system improves the feeling of immersion and is an element of the gameplay. For example breaking a lamp will allow the player to hide in the dark._

## HomeLAN Fed : Exclusives : Al Capone Interview : — First interview about Al Capone / Chicago 1930

- Interview subject: Martin Kuppe
- Original URL: http://www.homelanfed.com/index.php?id=11648
- Wayback capture: https://web.archive.org/web/20040604140142/http://www.homelanfed.com/index.php?id=11648
- Preserved source: [`04_martin-kuppe_a0d71ce3d9.html`](sources/04_martin-kuppe_a0d71ce3d9.html)
- Language: preserved as published; no translation applied

After exploring the Wild West in Desperados and the Middle Ages in Robin Hood, developer Spellbound heads for another historical period with Al Capone as their upcoming tactical strategy game will bring the 1930's gangster period to life. In the first interview about the game, HomeLAN got a chance to chat with Spellbound game designer Martin Kuppe to find out more about their plans for Al Capone.

**HomeLAN** \- First, were you happy with the reaction from gamers and critics for Robin Hood?

**Martin Kuppe** \- Yes, we were. Most of the reactions and reviews were quite positive. Things that have been criticized give inspiration to improvements in our future games.

**HomeLAN** \- How did the idea come about to make a game based on American 1930's gangsters?

**Martin Kuppe** \- We wanted a universe that is quite different from the Wild West and the Middle Ages, but war and SF scenarios are not very original as they are used in many games. So we had the idea of making a game in the Prohibition time.

**HomeLAN** \- How much historical research are you doing for the game?

**Martin Kuppe** \- We have read some books about the prohibition time: A Capone biography, a book about Eliot Ness, and "The Untouchables" by Eliot Ness himself. The storyline and some missions are inspired by the history, and most names in the game will be authentic. Moreover our artists are doing a lot of historical research on style and architecture of the Prohibition time.

**HomeLAN** \- What can you tell us about the storyline for the game?

**Martin Kuppe** \- The storyline is divided into two parts, corresponding to the mafia and police campaign. In the mafia campaign, the player plays the role of Frank "The Enforcer" Nitti, the right-hand man of Al Capone. Frank Nitti and his men conquer the city of Chicago quarter by quarter, until Al Capone reigns over Chicago. During these missions, they discover a conspiracy around murder and dirty money...

The police campaign begins when Capone is the king of Chicago. The player controls Eliot Ness, a federal agent in charge of the fight against the mob. The feds free quarter after quarter from the claws of Al Capone, and discover more about the conspiracy. At the end, there is a showdown against Al Capone, and the men at the origin of the conspiracy are uncovered. You can also play the police campaign first - in this case, the mafia campaign is a review on what happened before.

**HomeLAN** \- What playable characters will you be able to use in Al Capone?

**Martin Kuppe** \- For both campaigns there will be about 15 playable characters, each of them having his own character, abilities and look. They will evolve by upgrades or by gaining experience, like in RPGs. The principal characters will be Frank Nitti for the mob and Eliot Ness for the feds.

**HomeLAN** \- What sort of locations will be seen in the game?

**Martin Kuppe** \- The majority of the missions will play inside buildings. All locations will reflect the typical feeling of the prohibition time period.

**HomeLAN** \- How would you describe the gameplay for Al Capone?

**Martin Kuppe** \- Al Capone is a team-based RTS like Robin Hood, but it provides more tactical challenge as the strength of PCs and adversaries are more balanced. You can no more confront 20 adversaries with a lonely hero and hope to survive. Moreover, you can no more detect enemies outside the view volume of your PCs. In consequence, you will proceed more cautiously, as you do not know who is behind the next corner. When sneaking around, you may profit from shadow zones to hide. But sometimes gunfights are inevitable - when you are discovered, when you attack a room full of enemies and so on. In this case you should take advantage of cover, use your firearms and grenades optimally and take care of not getting out of ammo.

There are some differences between the gameplay in the mob campaign and the cop campaign. When controlling the gangsters, you may kill everyone you want without seeing your men going to prison - as long as there stay no witnesses! You can get rid of witnesses by bribing or killing them. But take care: If you leave too much innocent dead bodies behind you, the popularity of the mafia will drop and you get less new men. On the other side, the cops must be much more cautious with their guns:
When a cop kills a gangster without being attacked, he can loose his police mark. He has even not the right of arresting a gangster if he has no evidence against him. To get evidence, you must first interrogate witnesses and examine objects.

So, a typical mafia mission consists of the three phases "Approach - Strike - Take care of witnesses". A typical cop mission goes the other way round: "Take care of witnesses - Approach - Arrest".

**HomeLAN** \- What weapons will be used in the game?

**Martin Kuppe** \- There will be different types of firearms: Colt .38, Winchester, pump gun and the Thomson sub-machine gun, better known as "Tommygun". Then there will be multiple close combat weapons: Switchblade knife, baseball bat, knuckleduster and the medic's secret weapon: Chloroform. Furthermore you can use throwing knifes and pineapple grenades. Besides the weapons, there are other objects to interact with NPCs and PCs: Ropes or handcuffs to tie up adversaries, medipacks to heal your comrades, money to bribe witnesses, and so on.

Note that now, we have real inventories and every PC can in principle use every weapon. His capacity in using this weapon depends on his abilities.

**HomeLAN** \- What other unique gameplay elements will be used in Al Capone?

**Martin Kuppe** \- In critical situations, you can switch to the Slow Motion Mode: The game runs six times slower, giving you the time to react faster than your enemies. During slow motion mode, there are some nice motion blur effects, the music changes, and the sounds are adapted. You will even see bullets flying through the air.

**HomeLAN** \- What can you tell us about the graphics engine in the game?

**Martin Kuppe** \- The engine is an evolution of the Robin Hood engine: A 2D engine with 3D data to handle projectile trajectories, sight obstacles and so on. We have made some improvements to the engine: First of all the engine now allows recolouring the sprites "on the fly". This means that two humans can use the same sprite but in different colours of jacket, hat, shoes etc. In consequence every one of the 30 PCs will have his own unique look, although we can technically limit to 8 different sprites for the playable characters.

Based on this system the engine supports now also an adaptive illumination on sprites. In a dark corner the sprite will be much darker as besides a bright lamp - as long as the lamp has not been destroyed by a bullet or a well-placed hit with the baseball bat.

**HomeLAN** \- Will there be any multiplayer modes in Al Capone?

**Martin Kuppe** \- No. The gameplay is not adapted to make a multiplayer game with.

**HomeLAN** \- What is the current status of the game and when will it be released to stores?

**Martin Kuppe** \- We have finished the game design and started production. The game will be in stores before Christmas 2003

**HomeLAN** \- A while back it was announced that Spellbound would be working on a game called Vampire Hunter. What is the current status on that game?

**Martin Kuppe** \- The Vampire Hunter project has been buried, but we did not yet put a stake through its heart. Perhaps it will return to life some day… ;o)

**HomeLAN** \- Finally, is there anything else you wish to say concerning Al Capone?

**Martin Kuppe** \- I think Al Capone will please many kinds of gamers: Not only players who like infiltration games, but also fans of tactical games like Jagged Alliance 2. There will be three degrees of difficulty, and in easy mode also occasional gamers will enjoy the game.

Recently, we were pleased to receive the opportunity to learn a lot more about it in an extensive interview. This time, we follow up with Creative Director Jean-Marc Haessig who talks about the look and feel of his team's rendition of the legend and offers five new screenshots.

**Action Vault: When you set out to design the game, how did you decide on the overall look and feel you wanted for Robin Hood? What were the major considerations and influences that affected your choice?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** The world of Robin Hood is extremely rich, colorful and expressive. It's also a world that everyone knows. Our aim was to provide a new interpretation of the legend - in order to pay homage to it and, of course, to create a playful game world. My initial preoccupation was to fit in as much detail as possible so that players could enjoy the subject matter in its full glory. To do this, we first of all had to come up with new methods of data compression and storage.

That done, we were able to increase the size of the characters and backgrounds, as well as double the number of cutscenes. Then, to make the characters more expressive and lifelike, we opted for exaggerated proportions and animations. The buildings have also been deformed to make them look bigger and more full of character. Finally, we chose to use very bright, highly saturated colors. We wanted the game to look like the old Technicolor films - like the Robin Hood with Errol Flynn, or even the Disney version. Those particular interpretations of Robin Hood are cheerful and light-hearted and lend themselves very well to computer games.

**Action Vault: Since you've just mentioned a couple of them, to what extent if any were you either restricted or helped by previous depictions of the Robin Hood legend in movies and also in other media?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** The numerous existing interpretations of the legend of Robin Hood didn't hinder us in any way - quite the opposite. It's great fun playing around with clichés and people's collective memories. We tried to create a world that would be sort of a synthesis of all the different variations on the story, without getting hung up on the details of each one. Imagine that you dreamt of Robin Hood last night... Don't laugh, it happened to me about 10 years ago ;) Would you be able to describe EXACTLY how he looked, his clothes, hairstyle, beard? I don't think so - instead, you'd put together a sort of identikit picture of all the different Robin Hoods you've seen throughout your life. That's exactly how our characters were created - by sitting daydreaming in front of computer screens!

**Action Vault: In terms of the overall art direction, what high-level goals did you set for the members of the art team? To what degree have any of these changed or evolved since development began, and why?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** We had four main goals:
\- get players' hearts beating with excitement by creating a playful world with a high level of detail and emotion
\- innovate on the genre's gameplay in order to surprise players and suggest a new direction for action / infiltration games
\- provide a respectful but not slavish interpretation of the legend of Robin Hood and all its colorful characters
\- create locations with identities as unique as the characters so that players can immediately make out castles they've already visited and really feel like they're traveling through different areas.

These overall goals allowed us fix objectives for each team member but also gave each of them enough latitude to come up with individual suggestions. Then, all we had to do was occasionally discuss details and isolated issues to make sure we were all on the same wavelength. The advantages are obvious; the final result is generous and rigorous and ended up being a lot more than we were expecting... That's the beauty of real teamwork.

**Action Vault: While Robin Hood, the Merry Men, Maid Marian et al are figures of legend, the context in which they exist involves a real-world location during an actual period in history. How much research did you do, and how much emphasis did you end up placing on historical accuracy?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** We first of all collected as many books, films and other documentation - including a very useful medieval encyclopedia with architectural plans and engravings of fortified castles - as we could. Then, from all this information, we selected the bits that we felt we could adapt to our gameplay. This is very important; we're in the business of creating games, not plays or illustrated history books. We therefore need to be able to adapt the facts to our gameplay design, the techniques we use and the creative objectives we spoke about earlier. On the other hand, as we're in control, we can also choose to adapt our strategy to fit in with the constraints imposed by the story. There's no such thing as one solution to one problem. The end result is the product of numerous ideas and decisions - and a little bit of alchemy! With the same ingredients, another studio - or ourselves in a few years' time - would have come up with a very different vision.

## Action Vault - Robin Hood - The Legend of Sherwood Q&A — Robin Hood Q&A at ActionVault, continuation

- Interview subject: Jean-Marc Haessig

**Action Vault: With respect to the game's overall art direction and its actual graphics, what are the major challenges and obstacles you encountered? Did any real surprises crop up during development, either favorable or unfavorable?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** The only obstacles we face relate to resources. These include the game budget, project time, number of team members, as well as the machines we develop our games on and for. There's no limit to the imagination, but reality demands that you respect certain limitations - otherwise you'll get burned! But these are problems every developer faces, and even if we had machines with 20 tetrahertz processors and 120 GB of Ram we'd still be hoping for better!! That's why it's better to speak of challenges rather than obstacles.

We evolved new working methods for this project, and can take pride in the fact that our ETA was so accurate that we didn't need to avail of the usual built-in safety period and were able to announce that the game would be ready for Christmas instead of Easter!

I think that such an event is rare enough to warrant a mention. :)

**Action Vault: Since we know the game engine is an updated version of the one you created for Desperados, what considerations led you to use it again for Robin Hood? What modifications and enhancements have been added, especially relating to the graphics portion?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** We created the Desperados engine specifically for that game. At the time, we wanted to get away from isometric constraints based on the repetition of blocks and express ourselves as freely as possible with backgrounds made from one block. We invested a lot of time and energy in this engine, but were also aware that it was possible to push it further. For example, despite the freedom we had regarding the positioning of background objects, the isometric perspective used still created a feeling of artificiality. When Wanadoo suggested we use the Desperados engine to produce Robin Hood, we seized the opportunity to try out a few new tricks; for example, we deformed the buildings to give each one an individual look. This makes the graphics look more realistic and brings out the full effect of the imposing medieval buildings!

**Action Vault: What type of animation system are you using? What are the main benefits and the key improvements compared to the original implementation in Desperadoes?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** The animation system is based on sprites. We developed a tool which allows us to very quickly integrate renderings and process them image by image in order to control the speed and fluidity of movements with a minimum of images. Using sprites also allows for the same control over detail as over the background - to the very pixel - which seemed to us the best means of creating a coherent whole. Because of this - and because we increased the size by 125% - the characters in Robin Hood are very detailed, with realistic shapes and textures.

**Action Vault: What are the main features of the graphics engine? To what extent will it take advantage of the features offered by the latest generation of video cards? What resolutions are supported? To what extent can the player control the camera? What CPU and what type of video card will be required to see the game at its best?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** Robin Hood's graphics engine is based on data compression - which is very slow - and its subsequent decompression and display - which is very fast. Unfortunately, we can't take advantage of the features offered by the latest cards as these are increasingly dedicated to real-time 3D graphics. This is highly regrettable given what can be done in 2D. No 3D card would be able to reproduce graphics as richly detailed as Robin Hood's.
The engine supports resolutions of 640x480, 800x600 and 1024x768. It's possible to zoom from 50% to 200%. The ideal system spec would be a Pentium III with 128 MB of RAM and a resolution of 800x600. On slower machines, players can set their resolution to 640x480 and, for example, turn off alpha blits - which would only influence enemies' fields of vision but would have no effect on the backgrounds and characters.

**Action Vault: What aspects of the art direction and graphics in Robin Hood are you most pleased with, and why?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** Once again, our team has outdone themselves and the final product is much better than we initially hoped. It's a bit like when you order a coffee and get a nice chocolate with it without having to ask for it. Well, with our team, you get a blueberry muffin as well. :) I think the fact that a game is a labor of love can felt by people playing it. Synergy in a team is a wonderful thing and we hope that people will have as much fun playing our game as we had creating it!

**Action Vault: Is there anything else you think our readers might find interesting on the subject of the game's art direction, or possibly something you'd like to ask them?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** Once you've got a taste for chocolate with your coffee, you'll be disappointed if you don't get any... So the moral of the story is forget about the chocolate and you'll be much happier! ;)

**Whether they're compared to chocolate, blueberry muffins or any other treats, the visuals in Robin Hood - The Legend of Sherwood amply demonstrate the talents of the Spellbound Studios art team. We certainly look forward to seeing more in the coming weeks and also in the full game, which is projected for release within a fairly short time. And naturally, thanks to Jean-Marc Haessig for his interesting and informative answers to our questions on the topic.

At the same time his evil brother John Lackland tries to seize the crown and oppress the people with the aid of the Sheriff of Nottingham. Robin of Locksley, chased from his castle by the sheriff, opposes the prince and his henchmen. He hides in Sherwood Forest, finds a group of outlaws and begins to collect the ransom for himself – mainly by attacking convoys in the forest and robbing gold in the cities. He contacts two royalist barons and organizes the resistance against Prince John. The prince is not amused, he orders his barons to attack the castles of the royalists. But with the aid of Robin and his merry men, the royalists defend themselves and begin the counter attack...**

_Is it more inline with the original story or the “kiddy version” Disney put out?_

**Martin Kuppe: The story is inspired by the original ballads, but also by the later traditions up to modern movies, which are now an indispensable part of the legend. Remember that if we would only follow the five original ballads, there would be no Maid Marian, no king Richard, no quarterstaff fight, even no “take from the rich and give to the poor” – at least not the “give to the poor” part. But I can assure you that the story has nothing to do with the Disney version – for the simple reason that it’s me who wrote the story, and I have never seen the Disney film. :o)**

_What do you think the hardest part is working on a title that has a main character recognized by all ages throughout the world?_

**Martin Kuppe: The main problem is that everybody has his own idea of the main characters. This is even more valid for the secondary characters like Maid Marian or Little John, as they are less “standardized” than Robin himself. Friar Tuck, for example, is small and thick in our game, although many people may imagine him tall and strong. It is simply not possible to match the ideas of all people – unless you add a “Friar Tuck is tall and strong” button in the options. ;o)**

_What are some of the different areas we will visit – Sherwood Forest obviously but any other “famous” Robin Hood places?_

**Martin Kuppe: Besides the HQ in Sherwood forest and the three forest crossroads, the player can visit five towns and castles: Lincoln, Leicester, Derby, the city of York and, of course, Nottingham. All towns and castles exist in different ambiences - good weather, fog, night – which also influence the gameplay, as the enemies see less far in fog or night.
**
_How many levels will there be in the game? Is that how it is split up? Levels or areas?_

**Martin Kuppe: The game is composed of 37 missions that are played in the above-mentioned 8 locations. The missions are arranged in nonlinear order. Between the missions you find yourself in your HQ in Sherwood Forest. There you can assign team members to tasks like making arrows or bow training, select the next mission on a map and compose your mission team before the next mission is loaded.**

_Is it mission-based? If so, what are some of the missions?_

**Martin Kuppe: There are many different types of missions. Some examples:**

  * **Sneak into the castle of a good royalist baron to contact him, without killing any soldiers.**
  * **Save Stuteley and some peasants from the stocks**
  * **Participate at an archers contest at Nottingham that is, in fact, a trap set by the sheriff.**
  * **Prevent the forced wedding of Maid Marian and the evil Guy of Guisbourne.**
  * **Spy out a secret meeting between Prince John and his barons**
  * **Intercept a tax transport or an escort on a forest crossroad, by using many different traps prepared by the Merry Men. This kind of mission exists in many variants that are mainly how the player collects the ransom for King Richard.**
  * **Intercept a food or weapon transport to weaken the enemy forces before a battle**
  * **Introduce into an enemy castle during a battle in order to help the attacking royalist forces by opening a gate, disabling a squad of elite archers, lowering a drawbridge, killing an enemy general etc. In these kinds of missions, you can call allied soldiers for help.**

_When announced I pictured a Diablo-type game where you go from town to town “robbing the rich and giving to the poor” – is that accurate?_

**Martin Kuppe: Robin Hood is more a team-based infiltration game like Desperados or Commandos, with different special actions for every player character. When you start a mission, you find your mission team in a coin of the map and you can plan how to procede. The focus is not just on collecting gold and killing adversaries, but on reaching the mission objectives that are different from mission to mission – see above.
But action is not neglected. We added an easy-to-handle swordfight system – you make special strikes simply by drawing mouse traces on the screen. In fact you can always choose whether you prefer infiltration and sophisticated plans or swordfight action.
An important point is that the game rewards players who spare the lifes of their enemies. The less soldiers to kill in a mission, the more merry men will join your band afterwards. **

_What are some of the weapons we will have access to as Robin Hood? Does he have any special “abilities” or characteristics the other characters don’t?_

**Martin Kuppe: Every player character has some special actions. Robin Hood, for example, can shoot with his bow, knock out adversaries, throw a purse full of money to provoke a brawl among the adversaries, jump from roof to roof, climb on tendrils and loot disabled adversaries. Furthermore, every PC has his proper close combat weapon: Robin Hood fights with a sword in the towns and with a quarterstaff in the forest. Friar Tuck uses a mace, Little John a quarterstaff, Maid Marian a sword, Stuteley an axe and Scarlett a morning star. The other Merry man fight with swords, clubs and even pitchforks.**

**This is a team-based game, not a game about one-loneley hero who is familiar with every kind of weapon. The fact that every character has his proper close combat weapon encourages the use of the whole mission team.**

_Can you play as characters other than Robin Hood?_

**Martin Kuppe: Sure – you even must do so! Robin Hood is a team-based game, which means you have to combine the special abilities of several characters to reach your goal. Example: Stuteley diverts an adversary by throwing an apple, Robin knocks him out, Friar Tuck ties him up, and Little John carries him to a dark corner.**

**The coordination of actions is very easy due to our Quickaction system, which allows registering of up to three actions for every PC. With the space bar you execute the first action of every character. You can, for example, register two bowshots on different soldiers as first and second action for Robin. You make the same for Marian, but with other soldiers. Then you register as third action for both characters to run into a house. Now you approach Robin and Marian to the adversaries and press the space bar twice: Every character shoots two arrows on his predefined targets. You press the space bar again, and both characters run away.**

_Multiplayer – will there be one? If so how will you play? Everyone can’t be Robin Hood, right?_

**Martin Kuppe: No, there will be no multiplayer mode – it would not match very well to the gameplay. The game is team-based, but you often use the team members alternately: Robin jumps on the wall, sneaks to the gate and lowers the drawbridge, then Stuteley goes on to the east tower and lockpicks the gate, and so on. In single player mode this is no problem, but in multiplayer mode this would be boring for the players who have to wait.**

_What engine are you using for this game? If your own, why and what are some of it’s features?_

**Martin Kuppe: We used our own engine that has developped from the Desperados engine. The advantage of a house-made engine is that it is perfectly adapted to our needs. Furthermore we know exactly how it works. I coded a part of the animation system – when I see an animation, I sometimes guess where the problem is even before looking into the code.**

**It is a 2D engine which allows much more detailed backgrounds than any 3D engine. Just have a look at our levels: They are like paintings, without any texture repetition. This would be impossible in 3D! Behind each 2D level there is a 3D structure that is used to compute the geometrics of the view cones, the trajectories of projectiles and so on.**

**The characters are displayed as sprites having 16 different directions. The animations are very smooth; we have a sophisticated system of transitions to avoid jumps in the animations.**

_Is there going to be a demo or beta before release?_

**Martin Kuppe: Yes, there will be a demo with one mission that will soon be available.**

_What is your estimated released date?_

**Martin Kuppe: Robin Hood will be released in November 2002.**

_What games are you looking forward to this year?_

**Martin Kuppe: Mafia, Anno 1503, Praetorians.**

_What do you do for fun outside of gaming?_

**Martin Kuppe: Reading, watching movies, cooking for friends, writing. (Currently I am writing a nowel which re-tells the story of the Lord of the Rings – from the viewpoint of a Ringwraith.)**

Returning to England from the Crusades, King Richard the Lionheart has been taken prisoner. Held in an Austrian castle, his chances of escape or rescue are virtually non-existent. Of course, it will be possible to gain his freedom by paying a ransom, but the amount demanded by his captors is exorbitant. Neither is the regent, Prince John, eager to pay it. In his brother's absence, he has grown very accustomed to the power of the throne, and together with his supporters, a number of powerful Norman barons, he is actively lining his pockets through oppressive taxation, the proclamation of unjust laws and various other thinly veiled unscrupulous activities. The people have little reason for hope, but deep in the heart of Sherwood Forest north of Nottingham, one man and his followers refuse to bend and continue to live free.

Now late in development at Spellbound Studios, [Robin Hood - The Legend of Sherwood](http://www.robinhood-game.com/) will allow you to assume the role of the legendary hero, leading Little John, Friar Tuck, other members of the Merry Men band and Lady Marian in a fight for the common people, against the seemingly overwhelming forces of the tyrant. Described as a game of tactics and action, it will take the protagonists on an adventure through locations ranging from the famed forest to Nottingham Castle, York et al armed with bows and arrows, swords and their own steely resolve. Recently, we were pleased to receive the opportunity to learn a lot more about it in this extensive Robin Hood - The Legend of Sherwood Interview with Creative Director Jean-Marc Haessig of Spellbound and Producer Anne Devouassoux of European publisher Wanadoo.

* * *

**Action Vault: To take care of the preliminaries, please introduce yourselves and outline your respective roles on Robin Hood.

Jean-Marc Haessig:** I'm the Creative Director of Robin Hood at Spellbound. I'm in charge of the overall game design and the look and feel of our products.

**Anne Devouassoux:** I'm the Robin Hood producer at Wanadoo. I'm in charge of the overall quality of the game content and gameplay.
**Action Vault: What type of game is Robin Hood, and what kind of gameplay does it focus on? What genres, blend of genres or individual games have been influential in the game's design or development?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** Robin Hood will be a real-time tactics game like Desperados and Commandos, taking place in 12th century England. The idea is to provide real-time tactics fans an alternative to the World War II setting seen in so many other games in this genre. Although Robin Hood is a famous character throughout the world, there haven't been many games that deal with his legend in the past.

The player will have to do a lot of things. Sneak into mighty castles, ambush tax collectors in Sherwood Forest, figure out strategies to solve the missions (including who will join the team in a mission) and so on. The plot will be based on the legend of Robin Hood. The main objective of the game is to gather enough gold to ensure the safe return of King Richard.

**Action Vault: What is the basic structure of the game? How are the missions and objectives laid out? Does the player have to follow a linear sequence or will there be choices to make?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** The starting point for every mission is a map showing all the locations of the game. There will be several missions available at any given time, both story missions and random missions. In random missions, for example, the player's goal will be to rob a tax collector or weapons transport - both earning him money. In story missions, not only does the story advance, but he will also be able to collect more money through chests or moneybags. After all, getting the money for King Richard's return is the main object.

Given that there will be story missions and random missions available at the same time, the player will have to make the difficult choice of what to do next.

**Action Vault: What attracted you to create a game, and in particular a tactical one, based on the Robin Hood theme? And what are the main factors that have influenced the project?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** Robin Hood and his Merry Men are the ideal characters for a real-time tactics game. His name is known by virtually everyone around the world, and almost everybody knows at least some of the tales that are told about him. They're a small band of Merry Men in a struggle against the evil Prince John Lackland and his minions. Robin is not a great leader of a large army inside Sherwood Forest who fight an all out war against the Normans. His goals and methods are quite "subtle" - stealing taxes from Prince John, giving back the money and saving some of it for King Richard's ransom. And he achieves all of that with a small band of Merry Men. Then again, would Robin Hood have succeeded if he hadn't been accompanied by his Merry Men? If he couldn't count on the strength of Little John or the faith - and beer - of Friar Tuck, or the love of Maid Marian? We think that what makes the legend of Robin Hood so imaginative and popular is the wide range of characters it includes.

So when Wanadoo approached us with their concept for a Robin Hood game, we both put our ideas together and quickly had a game to show.
Research is always a big issue when designing a new game. There were a lot of things to learn -for the graphic artists as well as for our level and story designers. Our sources included the Internet, books on Robin Hood, medieval architecture and clothing, film adaptations like The Adventures of Robin Hood with Errol Flynn and last but not least Strasbourg, the city nearest to us. Its medieval town center offered inspiration for our 3D modellers who designed the houses and castles in Robin Hood. Finally, the cities and castles in Robin Hood are based on original maps and pictures of York, Derby, Nottingham, Leicester and Lincoln, but have been strongly adapted to meet our gameplay priorities.

**Action Vault: Since you've listed a number of the locations, how many will there be in total? And what were your most important goals when choosing and creating them?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** The missions will be set in the towns and castles surrounding Sherwood Forest: Nottingham, York, Lincoln, Leicester and Derby and of course in Sherwood itself. All in all there will be nine places to visit, some of them more than once and under different background conditions - day, night, fog, snow...

Our objective was to create strong identities for each location, to avoid confusion between the different castles, and also to provide more excitement and fun. So we have used all the known elements of medieval castles, towns and landscapes and have adjusted them to obtain a balance between gameplay, atmosphere and realism.

**Action Vault: Which of the famous characters will the player control and how will they differ from each other? And will there also be a variety of other Merry Men? And can any or all of the player characters improve during the game?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** There are six heroes and five types of Merry Men, all with their own characteristics. For example, the heroic Robin Hood is unquestionably the best archer in the game, but he is equally skilled at sword and staff fighting, as well as hand-to-hand combat. He is the most agile character and only he can fight duels with characters such as the Sheriff of Nottingham... and he can play on the enemy soldiers' greed by tempting them with purses of silver.

Little John fights only with a staff, though his great strength doubles its power. He can lift soldiers in armor up over his head, help his friends climb hard to reach places, push heavy objects such as tree trunks, and knock out his enemies like no one else. His piercing whistle is particularly useful for drawing soldiers to him.

Friar Tuck is the company's resident glutton! With him, any opportunity for having a quick snack is never wasted. He is also in charge of transporting the company's beer, which he uses to get enemy soldiers drunk. His favorite weapon is a mace, which leaves his victims with a worse hangover than any drink will. His natural caution has him tying up anyone he knocks down. His favorite trick is to throw a wasps' nest down a soldier's suit of armor!

Maid Marian is prepared to do anything to defend her country and help Robin. She is a skilled archer and swords-woman and is also well versed in the healing powers of plants. But her most precious gift is the ability to determine the identity of any soldier whose voice she hears, even if he happens to be on the other side of a wall! Like Robin, she never fails to search any corpse she comes across for money to put towards paying King Richard's ransom.

Will Scarlet is Robin's nephew, a young, happy go lucky character eager for action. His weapon of choice is a deadly flail and he doesn't balk at finishing off wounded soldiers - or at sneaking up on them and strangling them. He carries a large shield, which he can use to protect himself and another member of the company from enemy arrows. He is very agile and his prowess with the catapult enables him to strike enemies from a distance.

Stuteley is an old friend of Robin's. He is an expert at setting traps and ambushes and nobody can match his ability to capture netloads of enemy soldiers. He fights with a hatchet and also throws apples to knock out his opponents or else make them think that the local children are playing tricks on them. What's more, the apples only serve to attract Friar Tuck's wasps! One of his favorite tricks is disguising himself as a beggar and mingling with the unsuspecting soldiers...
The Merry Men are made up of local peasants who join Robin in Sherwood, bringing with them makeshift weapons such as pitchforks and clubs... They share some of the heroes' abilities but, unlike them, they have the advantage of being replaceable... Some can be sent out on missions while others remain behind in Sherwood producing weapons and food. Still others can be trained in the fine art of ambushing enemy soldiers. All the Merry Men are skilled at archery and hand to hand combat. Over the course of the game, their abilities evolve in keeping with the training they undergo and the battles they win.

**Action Vault: How did you choose the different weapons for the various characters? And can players expect to see more varieties than those you just mentioned?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** Each character has his own weapons, which we allotted in keeping with the well-known traits of the Robin Hood story. For example, Robin fights with a wooden staff in the forest and a sword elsewhere - he also keeps his bow and arrows with him at all times. Marian uses a bow and a sword. Little John only uses a staff, but his physical strength doubles its force. Will Scarlet is the most brutal member of the team and he uses a flail. Friar Tuck uses a mace while Stutely uses a hatchet. The other Merry Men use swords, pitchforks, clubs... Add the spears, halberds, two-handed swords and crossbows used by the soldiers and you have quite a collection of deadly weapons.

**Action Vault: What range of enemies will the player face, especially those drawn from the legend? What were your key goals when designing them and the AI that controls their behavior?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** Aside from key villains such as the Sheriff of Nottingham, Guy of Gisborne, Longchamp, etc. we also came up with a whole series of enemy soldiers. The soldiers all have different traits based on their roles, from simple foot soldiers to noble cavalrymen, and taking in lancers, halberdiers, archers, crossbowmen, officers and lords. What's more, each type of soldier exists in five variations, the weakest are dressed in blue while the elite wear black. Those in between wear yellow, orange and red. Those dressed in green are Robin's allies and fight by his side.

Our aim was to create an army with a credible, coherent hierarchy between officers and soldiers. It also includes more independent characters such as lords - these are proud individuals and won't join in battle with simple soldiers! Instead, they will observe the battle from a distance, only joining in when there are no soldiers left. Officers will summon soldiers in order to give them orders, and foot soldiers armed with shields will form lines to protect the archers behind them. The game's AI was entirely reworked in order to recreate the battle tactics of the time, such as sword, mace and hand to hand fighting!

**Action Vault: Will friendly or neutral NPCs play important roles in Robin Hood? And are there any other famous characters that will appear?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** You will encounter friendly NPCs all through the game, from defenceless peasants needing help (and earning you bonuses) to beggars who will provide information in return for small sums of money. Most of the legend's key characters, such as Ranulph or Sir Godwin, play an important part in the plot, as do Robin's sworn enemies - the Sheriff of Nottingham, Prince John, Guy of Gisborne, etc.

**Action Vault: While we touched on the topic earlier, please elaborate on the game's missions and your primary goals when designing them. How many are there, and how much do they vary in types and goals?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** Our aim is to provide as much diversity as possible over a solid gameplay base. We want to surprise the player and give him as much pleasure as possible. We therefore decided not to simply have the missions follow one after another, in a linear manner, but to create a real campaign with a map of the area around Sherwood showing castles and paths through the forest. Players can choose between several different missions at any given moment and can even select different teams for different missions. Campaigns are made up of about 40 missions and include rescue missions for recruiting new Merry Men, spying missions for finding out Prince John's plans, ambush missions for robbing the tax collectors, siege missions for attacking castles, missions for attacking military convoys passing through the forest and even a mission to prevent the marriage of Maid Marian to the despicable Gisborne!

Interactivity with the environment is very wide-ranging, from opening a drawbridge to knocking over a pile of wood, from setting traps in the forest with nets to climbing ivy-covered walls, as well as the bonuses and information hidden throughout the game's large maps.
Interaction with the enemy is of course more exciting, thanks to hand to hand combat against one or more opponents, and the various ways of killing, knocking out, trapping and generally annoying your enemies...

**Action Vault: Will Robin Hood include scripted events to advance the plot? Will there be many, and what degree of importance will they have overall?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** Generally, scripted events will only be found at the start of missions, in order to set the tone and provide links with the previous missions. **Action Vault: What engine are you using, and what makes it especially suited to the game? What major modifications and changes are you making, and what will the system requirements and recommendations for Robin Hood be?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** We took the motor used for Desperados as our starting point as it allowed for high levels of detail. We modified and improved it to make passages between missions shorter, the animation more fluid and the AI specifically adapted to this environment. We've improved the game interface, and also the quick-actions that were so popular with Desperados; this time round, players will be able to program up to three quick-actions per team member...

**Action Vault: The game doesn't seem to include multiplayer as a feature? Why did you choose not to incorporate it?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** No, there is no multiplayer mode. As in Desperados, the genre doesn't justify such a mode.

**Action Vault: When and how did the idea for the game initially arise? When did actual development begin, and what is the current status? When will Robin Hood be ready for release, and how confident are you about this date?
Anne Devouassoux:** The idea arose as we played Desperados Wanted Dead or Alive and Commandos. Many people around us were indeed fans of those two hits, and we had a lot of fun playing those games. At the same time, we were thinking about developing a game based on the theme of Robin Hood. So naturally, we mixed our pleasure for tactical games and our will to work in this universe, and decided to begin designing the concept of Robin Hood and defining its main features. We contacted Spellbound Studio and proposed the Robin Hood concept to them. They were really excited because the concept happened to correspond with the new concept they were currently working on!

The pre-production began last September. The pre-production and production phases are very short because the Robin Hood engine is based on the Desperados engine, with new features of course. The production cycle is 12 months long.

The game is presently at pre-master candidate, so I'm very confident in an end of the year release.

**Action Vault: Please tell our readers about Spellbound and its background. When and how was the Robin Hood team put together and where is it located? Who are the key members, and what backgrounds do you have making games?

Jean-Marc Haessig:** Spellbound was founded six years ago in Kehl, on the French-German border. Initially, we designed games for the German market and our first international title was Airline Tycoon. We then spent two years working on Desperados, increasing our team from four to 16 in the process. For Robin Hood, we increased our team to 22 people, allowing us to complete the game in a record 10 months. We're currently ironing out the bugs in the game and, of course, developing new projects.

Most of our team cut their teeth on Desperados, and our most senior programmer is Armin Gessert who has been programming since 1984 and who created Great Giana Sisters on the C64!

**Action Vault: Why will Robin Hood stand out from other action-oriented tactical games of the present time? What kind of gamer will find it most appealing?

Anne Devouassoux:** Our goal during development was fun and no frustration for the gamer. We analysed other games of this genre and put in new features like non-linear campaigns, combat mode, collection of items, several different kinds of missions, etc.

It's really up to the player to choose the way he wants to play. If he wants to play in a "classical way" (like he plays Commandos or Desperados), he can. If he wants to play in an action way, rather than a tactical way, he can. If he wants to let the AI manage the combat, he can. If he prefers to play the combat, he can. Hardcore gamers will play exactly the same way they are used to. And new players will enjoy the game because it's not necessary to be an experienced tactical/action gamer to have fun with Robin Hood.

The gameplay is a subtle mix between hardcore gameplay and "obviousness".

With three difficulty levels, we hope to be able to reach more players. We want players to get the full value for their money and with this in mind we take their ideas and comments into consideration - after all, nothing is as important as true emotion.

The legend of Robin Hood and his band of Merry Men stealing from the rich in medieval England and giving their goods to the poor has been told and retold countless times over the centuries and in many different formats. Later this year developer Spellbound Studios (best known for their Infogrames Wild West action-strategy game Desperados) will give their version of the legend in Robin Hood: The Legend Of Sherwood, their upcoming action-strategy title. HomeLAN got a chance to chat with Spellbound's creative director Jean-Marc Haessig along with Anne Devouassoux, the game's producer at Wanadoo, to find out more about Robin Hood which will be published in the US by Strategy First.

**HomeLAN** \- How did the idea for developing a Robin Hood game come about?

**Anne Devouassoux** \- The idea arised as we played Desperados “Wanted Dead or Alive” and Commandos. Many people around us were indeed fans of those two hits, we had a lot of fun playing those games. At the same time, we were thinking about developing a game on the theme of Robin Hood. Even if the legend of Robin Hood took place in the 14th century the theme is still very appealing and has great brand recognition.

So naturally, we mixed our pleasure for tactical games and our will to work in this universe, and decided to begin designing the concept of the game Robin Hood and to define its main features. Obviously we contacted the Spellbound Studio and asked them to work on the concept of Robin Hood. They were really excited because that concept happened to be similar to the new concept they were already working on!

**HomeLAN** \- There are many different game genres that could have been used for Robin Hood. Why did you decide to create the game as a tactical-action title?

**Jean-Marc Haessig** \- Robin Hood and his “Merry Men” are the ideal characters for a real-time tactical game. His name is known by virtually everyone around the world and almost everybody knows of at least some of the tales that are told about him. They’re a small band of “Merry Men” in a struggle against the evil Prince John Lackland and his minions. Robin is a great leader of a large army inside Sherwood Forest who fights an all out war against the Normans. His goals and methods are quite “subtle” – stealing taxes from Prince John, giving back the money and saving some of it for King Richard’s ransom. And he achieves all of that with a small band of “Merry Men”. Then again, would Robin Hood have succeeded if he hadn’t been accompanied by his merry men? If he couldn’t count on the strength of Little John or the faith – and beer – of Friar Tuck or the love of Maid Marian? We think that’s what makes the legend of Robin Hood so imaginative and popular is the wide range of characters it includes.
So when Wanadoo approached us with their concept for a Robin Hood game, we both put our ideas together and very soon had a game to show.

**Anne Devouassoux** \- The strength of the theme is the personality of the leader (Robin Hood) and his group (the merry men). It was really important for us to play Robin Hood as well as the other characters too and the theme fits with the different missions proposed by a tactical/action game (stealth, ambush mission, save people, attack of castles, etc).

**HomeLAN** \- How much research on the Robin Hood legend and on the game's time period did the team do?

**Jean-Marc Haessig** \- Research is always a big issue when designing a new game. There were a lot of things to learn – for the graphic artists as well as for our level and story designers. Our sources included the Internet, books on Robin Hood, medieval architecture and clothing, film adaptations like “The Adventures of Robin Hood” with Errol Flynn and last but not least Strasbourg, the city nearest to us. Its medieval town center offered quite a few inspirations for our 3D-modellers who designed the houses and castles in Robin Hood.

Finally, the cities and castles in Robin Hood are based on original maps and pictures of York, Derby, Nottingham, Leicester and Lincoln, but have been strongly adapted to meet our gameplay priorities.

**HomeLAN** \- What are some of your favorite interpretations of the Robin Hood legend?

**Jean-Marc Haessig** \- I’m a big fan of the Errol Flynn film – I like its energy, color and the character styles. Kevin Costner’s film took a more modern approach, which is also very interesting, though I found that, aside from Robin and Will, the other characters were a bit two-dimensional… In any case, I liked almost all of the versions of the Robin Hood legend, from cartoons such as Walt Disney to cameos such as Mickey’s or even the one in Shrek, etc. Robin Hood is a very strong, symbolic character who appears and reappears in different guises throughout our lives. We’re happy to be able to provide our own interpretation of the character and proud to contribute to the Legend of Robin Hood.

**HomeLAN** \- What is the actual storyline for the game?

**Jean-Marc Haessig** \- We’ve made some changes to the true Legend of Robin Hood, both to provide surprises for those familiar with it and to suit the needs of the gameplay. Overall, though, our version contains all the main elements of the Legend, starting with Robin’s return from the Crusades and ending with his duel with the Sheriff of Nottingham, taking in his encounter with the Merry Men, the archery tournament, Marian’s marriage to Guy of Gisborne, etc., along the way.

One example of the changes we’ve made is Robin’s meeting with Friar Tuck. In the Legend, they meet on the banks of a stream and Robin asks Friar Tuck to carry him across the water on his back. In the game, Tuck appears twice before he joins the team, and he is also to be found – strangely enough – on top of a keep…

**HomeLAN** \- What locations will be featured in Robin Hood?

**Jean-Marc Haessig** \- The missions will be set in the towns and castles surrounding Sherwood Forest: Nottingham, York, Lincoln, Leicester and Derby and of course in Sherwood itself. All in all there will be nine places to visit, some of them more than once and under different background conditions (day, night, fog, snow…).

There are also encounters in Sherwood Forest which allow Robin’s men to attack tax collectors and garrisons of enemy soldiers.

**HomeLAN** \- What playable characters will be in the game and what will their abilities be?

**Jean-Marc Haessig** \- There are 6 heroes and 5 types of Merry Men, all with their own characteristics.

For example, the heroic Robin Hood is unquestionably the best archer in the game, but he is equally skilled at sword and staff fighting, as well as hand-to-hand combat. He is the most agile character and only he can fight duels with characters such as the Sheriff of Nottingham… He can play on the enemy soldiers’ greed by tempting them with purses of silver.

Little John fights only with a staff, though his great strength doubles its power. He can lift soldiers in armor up over his head, help his friends climb up to hard to reach places, push heavy objects such as tree trunks, and knock out his enemies like no-one else. His piercing whistle is particularly useful for drawing soldiers to him.

Friar Tuck is the company’s resident glutton! With him, any opportunity for having a quick snack is never wasted. He is also in charge of transporting the company’s beer, which he uses to get enemy soldiers drunk… His favorite weapon is a mace which leaves his victims with a worse hangover than any drink will. His natural caution also causes him to tie up anyone he knocks out.. His favorite trick is to throw a wasps’ nest down a soldier’s suit of armor!

Maid Marian is prepared to do anything to defend her country and help Robin. She is a skilled archer and swords-woman and is also well versed in the healing powers of plants. But her most precious gift is the ability to determine the identity of any soldier whose voice she hears, even if he happens to be on the other side of a wall! Like Robin, she never fails to search any corpses she comes across for money to put towards paying King Richard’s ransom.

Will Scarlet is Robin’s nephew, a young happy-go-lucky character eager for action. His weapon of choice is a deadly flail and he doesn’t balk at finishing off wounded soldiers – or at sneaking up on them and strangling them. He carries a large shield which he can use to protect himself and another member of the company from enemy arrows. He is very agile and his prowess with the catapult enables him to strike enemies from a distance.

Stuteley is an old friend of Robin’s. He is an expert at setting traps and ambushes and nobody can match his ability to capture net-loads of enemy soldiers. He fights with a small ax and also throws apples to knock out his opponents or else make them think that the local children are playing tricks on them. What’s more, the apples only serve to attract Friar Tuck’s wasps! One of his favorite tricks is disguising himself as a beggar and mingling with the unsuspecting soldiers…

The Merry Men are made up of local peasants who join up with Robin in Sherwood, bringing with them makeshift weapons such as pitchforks and clubs… They share some of the heroes’ abilities but, unlike them, they have the advantage of being replaceable… Some can be sent out on missions while others remain behind in Sherwood producing weapons and food. Still others can be trained in the fine art of ambushing enemy soldiers. All the Merry Men are skilled at archery and hand to hand combat. Over the course of the game, their abilities evolve in keeping with the training they undergo and the battles they win.

**HomeLAN** \- Who will be the main enemies in Robin Hood?

**Jean-Marc Haessig** \- All the key villains from the Legend of Robin Hood such as the evil Sheriff of Nottingham, the lazy Prince John, the cruel Guy of Gisborne who wants to marry Maid Marian, as well as less well known characters such as Longchamp, Scathlock, the tax collectors and hordes of enemy soldiers.

**HomeLAN** \- What kinds of missions will be featured in the game?

**Jean-Marc Haessig** \- There are 3 different types of missions:

>
>
> * Robbery and Infiltration: here you must find your way into enemy castles in order to seize riches amassed by the Normans on the backs of the poor, free hostages, attend secret meetings, or prevent the marriage of Marian to the despicable Guy of Gisborne.
>
> * Castle Attack: here you must help the Saxons conquer an enemy-occupied castle. You must weaken strategic Norman posts in order to make an invasion by the Saxon army or Robin’s men possible.
>
> * Ambush: small missions in Sherwood in which you must stop the tax collector’s convoy by trapping and attacking its escort or weaken enemy troops on their way to sieges.

What you can expect are quite a lot of different mission types – from rescuing a captured man to ambushes in Sherwood Forest to big castle sieges, where Robin Hood has to complete some strategic goals before the attack can begin. The most important thing is that we leave most of the choices to the player. This freedom starts with the tutorials in Robin Hood’s camp in Sherwood and continues with the choosing of men to form groups for the missions.

**HomeLAN** \- How will combat be handled in Robin Hood?

**Jean-Marc Haessig** \- Because most of the fighting in Robin Hood is hand-to-hand combat rather then gunfights we decided to include a fighting system which allows more than just point and click on the enemy. By drawing a symbol with his mouse, the player’s characters will be able to perform some nice tricks like thrusts or sweeping blows against their opponents. Of course the fights can be left to the AI. But then… who would turn down the chance of fighting a villain on a torch-lit stairway?

**HomeLAN** \- What other unique gameplay elements will be featured in the game?

**Jean-Marc Haessig** \- We’ve made some innovations which will delight fans of the genre, such as the ‘shadow-men’ who move about on the map at the start of the game. Normally, in this type of game, all your enemies are visible on the map right from the start. Other games show nothing at all, just a fog indicating war. We’ve opted for a system which allows players tell there are enemies present, without allowing them make out the enemy’s identity, be he a simple soldier, a lord, an archer or just a civilian… Players will have to move their men close enough to actually see the other characters in order to find out who they are. But there are also other means of finding out: you can buy information from beggars, or climb up onto Little John’s shoulders in order to see further, or take advantage of Marian’s sixth sense which allows her guess the identity of enemies she can’t see… This new approach maintains the level of suspense while covering the map with anonymous characters.

Another example is the very popular quick-actions from Desperados. These have been improved and now allow players to program up to three consecutive actions per character. Now your plans can be as machiavellian as you want!

Other new developments include the possibility of choosing missions from a map of England, of putting together a different team for each mission, of producing food and arrows in Sherwood, of training for battle, allowing players improve their men’s abilities at archery and hand to hand combat, etc.

**HomeLAN** \- What can you tell us about the game's graphics?

**Jean-Marc Haessig** \- Our graphics artists have outdone themsleves! Our programmers improved the data compression and storage systems which allowed for more fluid character animations. The characters are also larger than those in Desperados. To give you an idea, we doubled the number of images for each character, giving more than a gigabyte of non-compressed data! The backgrounds are also more highly defined – you can even see the leaves moving on the trees… The only limit to the graphics engine is our artists’ imaginations, and the end result shows that they really enjoyed the freedom they had to express themselves, filling in every detail with love and passion.

**HomeLAN** \- At the moment, what is the status of the game's progress and when do you expect to complete the game?

**Jean-Marc Haessig** \- Production has finished and we’re now putting the finishing touches to it and ironing out bugs so the game will be perfect when it comes out in 3 months…

**HomeLAN** \- What's next on Spellbound's agenda following the completion of Robin Hood?

**Jean-Marc Haessig** \- We’re working on new ideas, based on different technologies, which we’ll be showing to our partners. We’re in the process of making prototypes and previews in order to let them judge the ideas’ potential for themselves. Of course, these projects are all still highly confidential but we hope to be able to announce them soon.

**HomeLAN** \- Finally, is there anything else you wish to say about the game?

**Jean-Marc Haessig** \- Once again, we’ve done our best to top our previous efforts and to give players as many hours of fun as possible. With 3 difficulty levels, we hope to be able to reach more players. We want players to get full value for their money and with this in mind we take their ideas and comments on board – after all, nothing is as important as true emotion.

**Anne Devouassoux** \- It’s really up to the player to choose the way he wants to play (non-linear campaigns, combat mode managed by AI or the player, tactical or action approach, etc). Our goal was to allow the player to have fun while not being frustrated and I think we’re accomplished that. Players will be able to decide this for themselved when the game comes out for Christmas 2002!

We all know Robin Hood. He's that legendary English rebel who spoke with an American accent. He resided in Sherwood Forest along with his band of Merry Men, robbing from the rich, giving to the poor, and getting into Maid Marian's knickers on the odd occasion. And he won a silver arrow at some point along the way because he was such a top archer.

Spellbound, which was responsible for the Wild West-themed Desperados, is working on a similarly-styled game based around Big Robin. The title will be set in 12th Century England, and will follow the exploits of Hood and his crew as they try to gather enough gold to ensure the safe return of the imprisoned King Richard. It's a mission-based, real-time tactical affair where players will be sneaking into castles, leaping onto unsuspecting taxmen and rescuing captives from the clutches of villains.

We managed to spot Lincoln green-clad Jean-Marc Haessig recently while journeying through Sherwood Forest, so we collared the creative director of Robin Hood to find out more about the game.

**What's Robin Hood all about? What's the idea behind the game, what is the player required to do, and what is the plot?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** Robin Hood will be a real-time tactics game like Desperados and Commandos, taking place in 12th Century England. The idea is to provide fans of real-time tactics games an alternative to the World War 2 setting of other games of this genre. And though Robin Hood is a famous character throughout the world, there hadn't been many games that dealt with his legend in the past.

The player will have to do a lot of things. Sneaking into mighty castles, ambushing taxmen in the Sherwood Forest, figuring out strategies to solve the missions (including who will join the team in a mission) and so on. The plot will be based on the legend of Robin Hood. The main objective of the game is to gather enough gold for the safe return of King Richard.

**Why have you decided to do a game based around Robin Hood and what sort of research have you done for the title so far?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** Robin Hood and his Merry Men are the ideal characters for a real-time tactics game. His name is known by virtually everyone around the world, and almost everybody knows of at least some of the tales that are told about this character. They're a small band of "merry men" in a struggle against the evil Prince John Lackland and his minions. Robin is not a great leader of a large army inside the Sherwood Forrest who fights an all out war against the Normans. His goals and methods are quite subtle – stealing the taxes from Prince John, giving back the money and saving some of it for King Richard's ransom. And he achieves all of that with a small band of Merry Men. Then again, would Robin Hood have succeeded if he hadn't been accompanied by his Merry Men? If he couldn't count on the strength of Little John or the faith, and beer, of Friar Tuck, or the love of Maid Marian? We think what makes the legend of Robin Hood so imaginable and popular is the various, really strong characters it tells about.

So when Wanadoo Edition approached us with their concept of a Robin Hood game, we both put our ideas together and very soon had a game to show.

Research is always a big issue when designing a new game. There had been a lot of things to learn – for the graphic artists as well as for our level and story designers. Our sources include the Internet, books on Robin Hood, medieval architecture and clothing, film adaptations like "The Adventures of Robin Hood" with Errol Flynn and last but not least Strasbourg, the town in our neighbourhood. Its medieval town centre offered quite a few inspirations for our 3D-modellers who designed the houses and castles in Robin Hood.

Finally, the cities and castles in Robin Hood are initially based on original maps and pictures of York, Derby, Nottingham, Leicester and Lincoln, but have been strongly adapted to our gameplay priorities.

**We understand that the game will be mission-based. Could you give us some idea of the missions that players will undertake, and what locales these will be set in?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** The missions will be set in towns and castles surrounding Sherwood Forrest: Nottingham, York, Lincoln, Leicester and Derby and of course in Sherwood itself. All in all there will be nine places to visit, some of them more than just once and with different ambiance backgrounds (day, night, fog, snow etc.).

What you can expect are quite lot different mission types – from rescuing a captured man to ambushes in the Sherwood Forrest to big castle sieges, where Robin Hood has to reach some strategic goals before the attack begins. The most important thing is that we leave most of the choices to the player. It begins with the tutorials in Robin Hood's camp in Sherwood and does not end in choosing the men who will form the group for the missions.

**How many people will there be in a squad at any one time, and how will players control this squad?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** There will be up to five people forming a group. The controls are similar to those in Desperados and other RTS titles. By point-and-click, the player guides Robin Hood and his men through the map. Each time he can perform an action or he finds an interactive item the cursor will turn into a corresponding icon. This very intuitive kind of interface was a great bonus in Desperados.

Because most of the fighting in Robin Hood is hand-to-hand combat rather then gunfights, we decided to include a fighting system which allows more than just point-and-click on the enemy. By drawing a symbol with his mouse the player-characters will be able to do some nice tricks like thrusts or sweeping blows against their opponents. Of course, the fights can be left to the AI. But then... who would not like to fight against a villain on a torch-lighted stairway?

**What characters from the legend are you including in Robin Hood?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** The splendid Will Scarlet, mighty Little John, lovely Maid Marian, the good-hearted Friar Tuck, and of course the heroic Robin Hood. Besides them there are four more playable characters that are not part of the legend, but will add some more detail and special abilities.

**Will characters have different abilities or skills? If so, could you tell us about these?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** Every character is able to wield a melee weapon. In the case of Robin Hood that will be a staff when he is in Sherwood or a sword when he infiltrates castles. And, of course, he uses his famous longbow. Aside from that the characters will have some special manoeuvres, items or skills that come in handy if the player decides to avoid fights and sneak his way to success.

**Robin Hood and his band of Merry Men were famous for hiding in Sherwood Forest in their Lincoln-green outfits. Will there be any stealth elements or missions in the game built around this idea?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** We will supply each of the characters with all the skills and items necessary so that they are able to succeed either by fighting their way through a mission or, if they like, by hiding and sneaking. It's up to the player to decide which way he wants to solve a mission. Of course, there will be missions where hiding is not the best of options and missions where you'd be a fool to try and fight – but you can try.

Take the ambush missions for example: You have to hide until the convoy has stopped by one of your traps. But you won't accomplish anything if you stay hidden in the woods. So you have to sneak into good positions to start an attack and use the traps at the crossroad to your advantage.

**Will players actually be able to indiscriminately rob from the rich whenever they desire, or will they be restricted by mission parameters?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** If the question is whether the players are able to roam the countryside and ambush every single noble in sight, the answer is no, there won't be such a feature. The starting point for every mission is a map showing all the locations of the game. There will be several missions available at any given time, both story missions and random missions. In random missions, for example, the player's goal will be to rob a taxman or weapons transport – both earning him money. In story missions not only the story will advance, but he will also be able to collect more money through chests or moneybags. After all, getting the money for King Richard's return is the main object.

Given that there will be story missions and random missions available at the same time, the player will have to make the hard choice of what to do next.

**Robin Hood looks like Desperados but in a different setting. Could you tell us about any new gameplay, graphical or sound elements that you're adding that didn't appear in the Wild West game?**

**Jean-Marc Haessig:** Only the basic technical elements of Desperados are left in Robin Hood. A new method of compressing graphical data gives us the possibility to double the number of animation steps for each character. Furthermore they grew in size about 25 percent compared to Desperados, which means that you'll see more detailed characters.

There are a lot of new gameplay elements in Robin Hood. For one, there will be several "amulets". You could say they are a kind of Joker – giving you, for example, one last chance if Robin is knocked unconscious and threatened to be killed. Then of course the fighting system I mentioned earlier. It adds a lot of possibilities to combat.

The new A.I. routines enable both PCs as well as NPCs to act more intelligently. Every action a NPC does is calculated with a variety of variables. Scripts are rarely used and only when necessary. An example would be: Robin and Will encounter a single guard. What happens next depends on the guard's abilities. A guard capable of handling both Will and Robin would attack right away. If the guard is standing atop the gate house for example, a script would prohibit him from doing that – it's not very realistic, that a guard would open the gates, just to fight to people outside. A more cautious guard would try to retreat in order to find help, or alarm an officer who will then gather all available men to hunt down the intruders.

**Why will Robin Hood appeal to gamers?**

Jean-Marc Haessig: It will appeal to the players because they will be able to play one of the most renowned and popular outlaws in history. And because it will be even better than Desperados. We bet that players will have very much fun with Robin Hood and his Merry Men!

[**Mischa**] - **Mischa Strecker** (Spellbound)
[**Yurg**] - **Yuri Bushin** (Freelancer)

[**Yurg**] Please, briefly describe main features of your project to our readers. And is it true what you project based on Commandos?

[**Mischa**] We were of course inspired by Commandos, but we developed our own engine for Desperados. With this foundation, we evolved it and basically created a new one for Robin Hood. The A.I. for example, has been rewritten from scratch and is now far more powerful than the routines we used in Desperados. New compression methods enabled us to increase the size of the graphics and animation of the characters. We also included a "fighting system", to give players control over the action in hand to hand combat.

[**Yurg**] What sources of inspiration do you rely upon when creating the game's characters - books, movies, other games, perhaps? Is Robin's figure created from scratch or he's a main hero of some literary work brought to virtual life by you?

[**Mischa**] Everything you mentioned. The legend of Robin Hood, a saxon nobleman who eventually became an outlaw is a fascinating story. He took from the rich to support the poor, fought for his people against the Norman oppressors and for the save returns of King Richard the Lionheart. This "noble thief" inspired a lot of people, who adapted the legend into games, movies, books, TV Shows and such. So we had a huge "archive" of Robin Hood fiction to study from and use it for our game.

[**Yurg**] What weapons will our noble bandits wield? What is the main aspect of the game - stealth, tactical puzzles, straightforward battles or something else?

[**Mischa**] In melee situations Robin Hood and his men use swords, staves, nets and such. Robin and Marian, of course, will be adept with the bow. His merry men will have special abilities, which include the handling of weapons. They will mainly fight with staves, bludgeons or… apples! The main aspect of the game is what the player wants it to be. If they like a more stealthy approach to the mission or if they want to force their way through the game is entirely up to them. There will be many ways to solve Robin Hood.

[**Yurg**] In recent press-releases, you mentioned "40 non-linear missions". Could you explain (or give examples) of how players will experience this non-linearity?

[**Mischa**] Robin Hood will feature a lot of different types of missions. For example, ambush, infiltration and rescue missions. Some will appear out of the line with the story. Such as an ambush on a taxman or a heavily guarded weapons transport. For this kind of missions, the player will be able to set up traps and he has the advantage of his "home turf" - the forest of Sherwood. All the missions can be chosen on a campaign map where several opportunities are present at any given time, instead the straight forward method of solving one mission after another in other games of this kind.

[**Yurg**] What tactical features you're going to bring from Desperados; what are the brand-new ones?

[**Mischa**] At first glance, the gamer will find some known features from Desperados. But he will soon realize that these features were enhanced and new ones were added. The "quick action" feature is a good example: In Desperados you were able to program a series of actions and activate it by pressing a key. In Robin Hood all player characters can memorize up to three quick actions. The new A.I. routines allow much smarter enemies. Not only do they fall in defensive formations if the odds are against them. They obey orders from superior officers. And these guys will be really tough. Over all, the new features will add more action and suspense to the game. The fighting system I mentioned above is an example for one of the new features in Robin Hood.

[**Yurg**] Desperados featured an amazingly well-written, melodic soundtrack that I kept listening even after beating the game. What music will accompany us in robbing money-bags? Can we hope to enjoy some ear-pleasing Scottish tunes?

[**Mischa**] We still have a lot of work to do with the soundtrack. As for now, we are still experimenting with different kinds of medieval and music and traditional tunes. Rest assured, that the soundtrack will be as enjoyable as it was in Desperados.

[**Yurg**] Where will Robin's adventures take place? And, most importantly, will you include some legendary stories of Hood's bravery or write new ones?

[**Mischa**] It will take place in the Sherwood Forrest, the city of Nottingham and the castles and cities surrounding the Sherwood Forrest. We wanted Robin Hood to be exciting and fun, so we mixed the original legend with stories from Robin Hood movies, books and TV-shows and spiced it with our own ideas. All in all, the story outline will be recognized as the story we all know and love.

[**Yurg**] Is there a global storyline binding all missions together and if yes, then who's The Ultimate Big Bad Guy?

[**Mischa**] Yes, one of the most appreciated features of Desperados was the storyline. But this time, the story will not be linear like in Desperados for example. People, who play Robin Hood, will experience a story that will be transported through the missions and in between missions. There are quite a few bad guys, the cunning Sheriff of Nottingham, Prince John, the evil brother of Richard the Lionhearted and Guy de Gisborne to name a few.

[**Yurg**] Judging by the looks of RH's conceptual artwork, your game is going to be flavoured with humor. How you'd define Robin Hood - is it a serious or funny title?

[**Mischa**] A bit of both. Robin Hood will be a serious strategy/tactics game, but there is just no way not to include some funny details or actions. We think a game must be enjoying playing - and you wouldn't enjoy a game without any funny elements or small jokes that'll make you smile in it, would you?

[**Yurg**] A couple of questions concerning the game's "ñast". How would you describe each of 5 characters available to player in Robin Hood? What are their distinctive traits, abilites, "pros" and, maybe, "contras"?

[**Mischa**] I think Robin Hood and his merry men speak for themselves, and after all - let us keep some surprises until the release of the game :) All I will say now is that there will be distinctive abilities for each of the main characters, and there will be many more than five playable characters...

[**Yurg**] The game takes place in The Dark Ages, so we wonder will Robin Hood feature some serious "mano-a-mano" swordfights? How do you plan to implement such feature? What will be the rules of these duels?

[**Mischa**] Of course there will be dazzling swordplay, merry men attacking with staves, knives and swords. The rules of combat are not easy to explain. They depend on the characters experience, a few abilities (courage, dexterity and others), and the kind of weapon they wield. The exciting thing about the combat system is: It's the Player's choice if he lets Robin and his men do the job, or he takes control of them.

[**Yurg**] Finally, some standard stuff. Are there any sequels planned, will there be a demo and what is the expected release date?

[**Mischa**] As of now, we don't plan a sequel. But then, who knows what the future bears for us? The scheduled release of Robin Hood will be in the third Quarter of 2003.

[**Yurg**] Thank you very much for interesting conversation. I wish great successes to you and your colleagues!

R

obin Hood heißt das neue Werk der deutsch/französischen Spieleschmiede Spellbound, die vor allem mit der Wirtschaftssimulation „Airline Tycoon" sowie dem Taktikhit „Desperados" bekannt geworden ist. Wieder soll es ein Spiel der Commandos-Machart werden, jedoch mit einer frischen Ideenbrise garniert. **Krawall:** Spellbound's letztes Werk "Desperados" sorgte vor allem bei der deutschen Fachpresse für ordentlich Aufruhr, konnte aber mit seiner Verkaufsperformance nicht alle Erwartungen erfüllen. Wie betrachtet Ihr rückblickend den bisherigen Lebenszyklus des Titels ?

)

**Eine Location-Frage**
Neben dem berühmt berüchtigten Sherwood Forest bilden Prachtvolle Kathedralen und mittelalterliche Städchen die Kulisse für Robin Hood.

**Krawall:** Für Desperados war eine Reihe von sog. "Webisodes" sowie ein Add-On im Gespräch. Was ist daraus eigentlich geworden?
**M. Strecker:** Damit stellst Du eine Frage, die in unserer Top Ten durchaus einen Spitzenplatz einnimmt. Leider kann ich Dir nichts anderes sagen, als „An einem Add-On für Desperados arbeiten wir zurzeit nicht!"

**Krawall:** Kommen wir nun zu "Robin Hood", Eurem jüngst enthüllten Projekt. Seit wann befindet sich das Spiel in der Mache, und wie seid Ihr auf die Idee gekommen, diesen Mythos aufzugreifen?
**M. Strecker:** Am Spiel „Robin Hood" arbeitet unser Team seit Oktober 2001. Die Idee kam gleichermaßen von Wanadoo, als auch von uns. Wanadoo war letzten Sommer auf der Suche nach einem Entwickler, den sie mit einem Robin Hood-Spiel betrauen konnten. Weil ihnen „Desperados" gefiel, kamen sie auf uns zu.

**Krawall:** Was hat Euch bislang inspiriert, wo holt Ihr Euch Ideen für Story & Missionen ?
**M. Strecker:** Inspiriert wurden wir einerseits durch die Legenden und Geschichten um Robin Hood, andererseits durch die Verfilmungen im Kino und TV Serien – wobei ich die Serie, die zurzeit bei RTL läuft, ausschließen möchte. Die Quelle, aus der vor allem unsere Grafiker schöpfen konnten, war die Stadt in unser Nachbarschaft: Strasbourg und vor allem ihr mittelalterlicher Stadtkern. Außerdem existieren ja sehr viele Zeitdokumente, Wandteppiche und Farbtafeln, welche die Kleidung, Waffen und die Architektur des Mittelalters sehr gut dokumentieren. Aus diesem sehr bunten Mix haben wir die Rahmenhandlung und Lokalitäten gestrickt, auf der die Leveldesigner dann die einzelnen Missionen ausarbeiten können.

**Krawall:** Kannst du uns ein Beispiel für eine besondere Mission des Spiels geben ? Bastelt Ihr die Kampagne aus den allseits bekannten Bausteinen (Infiltrieren, befreien etc.) zusammen, oder erwartet den Spieler auch bislang unbekanntes ?
**M. Strecker:** Eine „typische" Mission in dem Sinne gibt es nicht. Die Spieler werden bekannte Missionen spielen können, in denen Robin und seine Anhänger in Kirchen oder Burgen eindringen oder einen gefangenen Gefolgsmann aus einem Kerker befreien. Sie werden aber auch Hinterhalte im Sherwood Forest legen, Fallen aufstellen und an gewaltigen Burgbelagerungen teilnehmen.

**Krawall:** An welchem Part des Spiels werkelt Ihr gerade, und was ist Eurer Meinung nach die schwerste Hürde bei diesem Projekt?
**M. Strecker:** Zurzeit arbeiten wir hauptsächlich an den Grafiksets, dem Animationssystem und der KI. Das Kampfsystem sowie die meisten Aktionen sind bereits implementiert. Die schwerste Hürde haben wir wohl im Game-Balancing noch vor uns. Der Spieler erhält in „Robin Hood" vor und während der Missionen viele Freiheiten. Zudem besteht ja die Möglichkeit, einen Zweikampf direkt zu steuern. Das alles müssen wir in Betracht ziehen, wenn unsere Level Designer die Arbeit aufnehmen.

**Krawall:** Fass bitte kurz zusammen, was für eine Art Spiel uns erwartet - was hebt "Robin Hood" von seinen Genrekonsorten ab ?
**M. Strecker:** Euch erwartet ein Spiel mit dem Flair alter Hollywood-Streifen aus den vierziger und fünfziger Jahren und actionreichen Duellen. Es fordert vom Spieler sowohl Weitblick als auch taktisch und strategisch kluges Vorgehen. Eine KI, die gegenüber Desperados deutlich verbessert wurde. Und auch mehr Möglichkeiten die Missionen zu lösen und mehr Freiheiten im Vorfeld einer Mission. Dadurch, und durch den einstellbaren Schwierigkeitsgrad, wird das Spiel insgesamt einfacher als Desperados, bietet aber immer noch genügend Herausforderungen für Freaks.

Was „Robin Hood" von den Genrekollegen sehr stark abhebt, ist das Thema und der Gameplay-Mix. Die Legende von Robin Hood ist sehr weit verbreitet und jeder kann sich etwas darunter vorstellen. Es weicht ein wenig von dem Standard Taktik/Strategie = Kriegsspiel ab, auch wenn natürlich auch Robin Hood kleinere und größere Kämpfe ausfechten muss. Der Hintergedanke ist der: die „Helden in Strumpfhosen" töten nur im äußersten Notfall und meistern im Grunde jede noch so schwierige Situation lieber mit Köpfchen und Geschick. Denkt nur daran, wie viel Kronleuchter, Teppiche und Kerzenständer und andere Gegenstände im Laufe der vielen Verfilmungen dran glauben mussten. Genau das wollen wir im Spiel vermitteln. Kopf vor Fäusten. Auch wenn wir dem Spieler die endgültige Entscheidung überlassen, ob er sich seinen Weg durch das Spiel kämpfen will, oder eine eher heimliche Vorgehensweise bevorzugt. Davon abgesehen enthält „Robin Hood" sehr viel mehr Elemente aus anderen Genres, als es auf den ersten Blick erscheinen mag. Die Hauptcharaktere gewinnen zum Beispiel mit jeder Mission an Erfahrung. Der Spieler darf – wenn er will – die Schwertkampf-Duelle aktiv mit der Maus steuern und muss mit einem Auge seine Finanzen im Blick halten. Denn schließlich war es Robin Hood, der das Lösegeld für Richard Löwenherz „zusammensammelte".

**Krawall:** „Desperados" konnte dem Vergleich mit Pyro's "Commandos" nicht aus dem Weg gehen. Habt ihr keinerlei Bedenken, das zwischen "Robin Hood" und ähnlichen Genrevertretern schnell die üblichen Parallelen gezogen werden, und das Spiel damit teilweise in den Schatten anderer gerät ?
**M. Strecker:** Nein. Wir freuen uns über den Vergleich mit „Commandos". Schließlich enthalten Commandos’ und Desperados’ Konzepte und Ideen des jeweils anderen Spiels. Beispielsweise führten wir in Desperados lebendige Dialoge als Mission-Briefing ein und einen Affen, um Gegner anzulocken. Auch „Commandos 2" enthielt diese Dialoge - und eine Ratte. Umgekehrt war natürlich „Commandos" eine Inspiration für „Desperados". Das „Robin Hood" untergeht oder in den Schatten eines Mitbewerbers gerät, befürchten wir nicht. Wir sind davon überzeugt, dass wir mit „Robin Hood" dem Taktik-Genre wieder einmal neue Impulse geben werden.

**Krawall:** Haben sich Taktikspiele dieser Machart nun als klare Stärke Eures Unternehmens profiliert, oder können wir in der Zukunft auch komplett andere Titel - z.B. ein reinrassiges Actionspiel - aus dem Hause Spellbound erwarten ?
**M. Strecker:** Durch „Desperados" und jetzt auch „Robin Hood" haben wir sehr viele Erfahrungen über Taktik-Spiele gesammelt, aber wir wollen uns nicht auf ein bestimmtes Genre festlegen lassen. Spellbound hat noch einige, sehr interessante Konzepte in der Schublade. Und auch „Robin Hood" wird sehr viel mehr als ein reines Taktik-Spiel werden. Lasst Euch einfach überraschen.

)

**Schick und übersichtlich**
Eine Minimap à la Desperados darf nicht fehlen. Bereits diese frühen Screenshots lassen auf einen Optik-Leckerbissen Marke Commandos schließen.

**Krawall:** Bislang erschienen Eure Titel allesamt bei Infogrames, nun habt Ihr für das "Robin Hood"-Projekt bei Wanadoo unterschrieben. Welche Beweggründe stehen hinter diesem Deal ?
**M. Strecker:** Wie bereits erwähnt wurde Wanadoo durch „Desperados" auf uns aufmerksam. Ihnen gefiel die Engine und die Liebe zum Detail, die in Desperados steckte und sahen in uns das ideale Entwicklerstudio für das „Robin Hood"-Spiel, das sie zu der Zeit realisieren wollten. Ungefähr zur selben Zeit reiften bei uns Pläne für ein eigenes „Robin Hood" - es passte einfach. Wir bringen unsere Ideen und Vorstellungen mit ein und kombinieren sie mit dem Konzept von Wanadoo.

**Krawall:** Ist ein Mehrspielermodus, z.B. mit einem Schwerpunkt auf dem gemeinsamen Durchspielen der Solomissionen, angedacht ? Und wie schaut's mit einem Editor aus ?
**M. Strecker:** Nein. Ein Mehrspieler-Modus verträgt sich einfach nicht mit dem Gameplay von „Robin Hood". Die Idee, dass mehrere Spieler gemeinsam eine Mission lösen, klingt im ersten Moment verlockend. Aber wir glauben, dass die Frusterlebnisse das gemeinsame Spielerlebnis für alle Beteiligten vermiesen würde, weil ein kleiner Fehler eines der Mitspieler den Ausgang einer Mission ruinieren könnte. Außerdem würden die Mitspieler erst einmal um das Recht, Robin Hood führen zu dürfen, streiten. ;-)

**Krawall:** Wie wir alle mitbekommen haben, werkelt Ihr derzeit auch noch an einer "Deluxe"-Version von "Airline Tycoon". Kannst du uns in ein Paar Takten erzählen, welches Eisen ihr hier im Ofen habt ?
**M. Strecker:** Deluxe Version klingt gut ;-) „Airline Tycoon Evolution" war als zweites Add-On zu Airline Tycoon und Airline Tycoon – First Class gedacht. Da zwei der neuen Features, nämlich dem „Aircraft Constructor" und dem Sicherheits-Büro, auch in den alten Missionen eine Fülle von neuen Lösungsmöglichkeiten eröffneten, entschieden wir uns für ein eigenständiges Spiel.

**Krawall:** Wir bedanken uns für das Gespräch und wünschen Euch frohes und erfolgreiches Schaffen!

## Worthplaying | 'Robin Hood The Legend of Sherwood' - Developer Interview — Robin Hood: The Legend of Sherwood developer interview at WorthPlaying

- Interview subject: Martin Kuppe
- Original URL: https://worthplaying.com/article/2002/10/30/interviews/6576-robin-hood-the-legend-of-sherwood-developer-interview/
- Wayback capture: not resolved
- Preserved source: [`13_martin-kuppe_e8fde49b11.html`](sources/13_martin-kuppe_e8fde49b11.html)

**1\. Who has the honor to talk to us? State your name, rank and occupation!**

_My name is Martin Kuppe. I am one of the two game designers of Robin Hood, the other one is Jean-Marc Haessig. I am a mathematician, and he is an artist – this cooperation works very well. Furthermore I am a programmer; I coded the AI for Desperados and for Robin Hood. Finally I also made the level design for some of the levels._

**2\. What gave you the ideas to make a Robin Hood game? Tell us a bit about the story.**

_When we finished Desperados, we thought about some game ideas we could realize with this engine. Robin Hood was one of our favorite ideas. Some time later, Wanadoo contacted us and asked “Hey, do you want to make a Robin Hood game with us?” Thus, with two parties having the same idea, we gladly started the project._

_Robin is a very popular and likeable man, and he is an ideal hero for a computer game. In the legend, Robin and his merry men fight the soldiers of the evil Sheriff of Nottingham, sneak into their castles and rob gold from the rich - a concept which matches very well with team-based gameplay.
I will not tell you anything about the story here – for if I do so, I will not know what to answer on question 11. :o)_

**3\. How many people can you control/have in your party at once?**

_You can have up to five people in your party at once. Before each mission (up to the first two missions) you find yourself in Sherwood where you can choose and equip your mission team and assign people to tasks like arrow production or sword training._

**4\. With the lack of multiplayer, how are you planning to keep the player playing and interested in buying this product?**

_First there are different ways to solve each level – you can sneak around the enemies, knock them out and hide them in a corner, fight them with the sword, shoot them up with the bow and so on. Secondly there are three levels of difficulty: When you solve the game in easy mode, you could play it again in medium or hard mode. Thirdly, you have the choice which PC you want to use in which mission – the same mission will result in a completely different gameplay when you play it with another mission team. Finally there are a lot of little secondary quests hidden in the levels, including the seven crown jewels that can be found in different missions. When you have collected all of them, there will be a … No, I won’t tell you everything?_

**5\. Robin Hood uses a similar detection system as the Commandos franchise. What have you changed/upgraded to the concept for Robin Hood?**

_The detection system is quite similar to the system in Commandos and Desperados. But we added a lot of other features which were not in Desperados: Selection of the next mission and of the mission team, management of ammo production, hidden enemies, the fighting system, an improved AI and much more._

**6\. Do enemies drop better armor/weapons, allowing your characters to acquire better stuff/upgrade?**

_Each character has his own specific weapons and additional special actions, but you can improve their fighting and shooting abilities, either by gaining experience in missions or by training in Sherwood._

**7\. Upon entering a town, what can you do? Can you rob people, buy/trade/sell items?**

_Robin Hood is a robber, not a trader. He can rob gold from the rich, either during the city missions or in special ambush missions in the wood. The latter missions are really fun: You can use a lot of traps that your people have prepared in the wood, like nets to catch enemies, rocks falling down on them, pits, men hidden in trees or beyond leaves and so on._

_The only thing you can buy is information: When you give gold to a beggar, he will tell you what soldiers are behind the southern gate, where to find Lady Marian or how to be introduced into the citadel._

_In the castles, the main goals depend on the mission; in general it’s not about gold. You have to free friends from the gallows, spy out secret meetings, save Marian from her forced marriage with the evil Guy of Guisbourne, and prepare the castle for an enemy attack during a siege and so on._

**8\. Will your units experience fatigue or hunger during their travels and fights? And what ways can they regain their strength?**

_The most important score is, of course, your life points. Some characters can restore their lifepoints by eating; other ones can heal other characters. During combats, there is a second value: Your energy. When you make special hits like a circular strike, you loose energy. When energy reaches zero, the character is out of breath and cannot do any strikes or parade for about a second. In this moment he is very vulnerable._

**9\. Will the game world be on solid world like Diablo? Or will each mission take you to a new place?**

_The game does not happen in a continuous world, it is based on locations. There are 5 castles and cities in the game, three wood levels for ambush missions and your headquarters at Sherwood. Each castle can be seen in different ambiances - day, fog and night – and three of them also during a siege of allied forces._

**10\. Why did you stick to a 2D engine? What was it that a 3D engine did not offer?**

_The main advantage is that the levels can be much more detailed than in any 3D game: No repeating textures, no simplified geometry - you will have the impression of playing in a huge beautiful painting. Our artists really did a great job with the level graphics._

_Note that logically, there is a 3D structure behind the 2D graphics. That means that things like view cones, arrow trajectories are calculated correctly in 3D._

**11\. Can you tell use about the Robin Hood story you plan to use? Will it be exactly like the story shown in the movies/books?**

_The story is very close to the Robin Hood legend as it has developed from the very first ballads till modern movies: Robin of Locksley comes back from the 3rd crusade and finds his lands confiscated by the Sheriff of Nottingham. King Richard Lionheart is not yet back from the crusade, and his brother John seems not to be able to control his subordinates. The sheriff takes advantage of his weakness to oppress the people and imposes high taxes. With the aid of an old friend, Stuteley, Robin decides to found a group of rebels to fight the Sheriff. But then he discovers that things are even worse…_

**12\. What is the current state of the game? When can we expect it in stores?**

_The game is finished and has been sent to the replication house. It will be in the stores by mid November._

**13\. Will there be any kind of leveling up as you progress through the game?**

_The player characters will have sword fighting ability and shooting ability which can increase during the game, as mentioned before. The sword fighting ability determines how good the character fights for himself when he is not controlled by the player. The shooting ability determines the precision of his shots._

_Furthermore, you will get more characters during the campaign, as Will Scarlett, Friar Tuck or Maid Marian, with new abilities. So you are able to confront the stronger enemies you will confront in the higher levels._

**14\. Since it’s a 2D engine will there be a fixed angle all the time or can you switch views to show the other side of a building/town?**

_The view angle is fixed. We constructed the levels such that the characters are rarely hidden behind obstacles, and when it happens, you can display their outline with the Shift Lock key. There are many tactical 3D games where you can turn the camera – but you never do it because it’s confusing. Did you play WarCraft3? I turned the camera only at the beginning for the “wow”-effect, but not later while playing the game…_

**15\. What kind of enemies will Robin Hood and his merry men encounter besides The Sheriff of Nottingham and his goons?**

_There are several types of soldiers: Spearmen, halberd men, shield bearers, crossbowmen, archers, officers, knights and riders. Each type exists in different levels, represented by colours, from weak blue soldiers to the black guard. Furthermore there are some “noble” enemies which can only be killed in a duel with Robin: The evil Guy of Guisbourne, the cruel Scathlock, the schemer Longchamps, and of course the Sheriff of Nottingham._

**16\. How many game hours do you expect Robin Hood to offer the serious gamer?**

_To play the campaign once, you will need about 30-50 hours, I guess, depending on your playing style. But as I said, you can play the campaign more than once, thanks to the difficulty levels, the non linear campaign, and the different ways to solve each mission._

**17\. Are you planning a sequel or any other title base in the Robin Hood universe?**

_Currently we are not planning a sequel or something like this. But who knows?_

**18\. Have you learned anything from the community feedback to add/optimize the game?**

_Many players said that Desperados was too hard for occasional players. We think that Robin Hood is easier to play; furthermore it provides three degrees of difficulty._

**19\. Finally is there anything you would like to add now that the demo is out?**

_I think the game has reached a very great level and we’re very satisfied with it.
After the demo we concentrated on bug fixing, which includes as few changes as possible. Every change can introduce new bugs, and it is important to deliver a game with as few bugs as possible. Who wants a game that crashes every time because of some last-minute features?_

Interview - Willem Drijver: Robin Hood by Timo Paul

1. Hi Willem, thank you for agreeing to this interview. Can you please introduce yourself and tell us a bit about yourself?

Thank you for bringing us WhatIFF? magazine and I am more than happy to contribute with this interview. 2. How did you start using the Amiga?

Like many in Amiga community, my first real computer was a Commodore 64, where I spent countless hours playing (and pirating) Games and learning Basic programming. At the time I started my study I started using Atari ST (practice what you preach J), but my heart was stolen when the Amiga 1000 was introduced and from there I was lost forever. I do still have my first A1000 right here beside me, revived with an Apollo Firebird. My primary use for Amiga 1000, besides playing games of course, was coding, hacking and doing some amateur-level multi-media stuff.

3. Have you programmed games for the Amiga in the past?

I honestly have to admit that I am a lousy coder and although I did pass all the exams on Assembler, Pascal, C, etc I could never reach the level of some of my coding friends around me who were very productive in cracking games and/or creating their own demos. I was more intrigued with the commercial possibilities, although I always stayed in touch with my engineering roots (I actually was one of the first batch of Microsoft Engineers (MCSE) here in Holland.

4. You're currently working on converting Robin Hood: The Legend of Sherwood for the Vampire V4+ series of accelerators/standalone. Is this your first game for the Vampire platform?

Yes. The project of porting "Robin Hood" to Apollo V4 Series started from my personal wish to learn more about how real professional games are coded. When I restarted learning to code for Amiga on Apollo Vampire V2 and later V4 series, I admired even more than before the beauty of the Amiga architecture. The evolution by the brilliant mind of master Gunnar of that architecture to our current Apollo 68080 CPU and SAGA chipset is simply amazing, so when I was presented the opportunity to explore the fantastic game "Robin Hood: Legend of Sherwood" by Spellbound Studios and bring it to life on Apollo V4 I grabbed this with both hands.

5. What made you choose Robin Hood: The Legend of Sherwood?

This was a case of the right time and moment. On the one hand, I was looking into the coding structures of (Amiga) Games and on the other hand I stumbled by coincidence on Robin and his merry men. I did not know the game myself at all, so I watched some video clips and I immediately was captured by the beautifully detailed graphics and the whole immersive feeling of the game. After some initial studying, I decided to really make an effort to not only port the game but to also adapt it to Amiga style and of course optimize it using the powerful Apollo V4 features.

6. Why did you choose to develop for the Vampire platform instead of classic Amiga, OS4, or Morph OS?

Well, the Apollo Vampire V2 was the reason I rediscovered Amiga as it brought my A1000 back to life and I could re-live all my memories playing games. From there I restarted to take an interest in coding with ASM for the m68k and C for AmigaOS. The more I got into coding the more I understood of the great work that had been done developing the Apollo 68080 AMMX CPU and the SAGA special chipset. When the V4 arrived I had just joined the Team and was responsible for ApolloBoot as my personal project and later also coordinating ApolloOS development within the team. With all the additional features the new V4 Series offered I really started to dive into writing Apollo V4 optimized code. For me personally there is no feeling with either OS4 or MorphOS (this is not a qualification!).

7. What was the most challenging aspect of porting Robin Hood to the Vampire?

Robin Hood: Legend of Sherwood was released in two editions, the first was targeted for Windows and required 200Mhz+ Intel CPU to run. Development continued and in 2005 there was a release for OSX, which was again later in 2012 ported to Linux. For this version the requirements were lifted to 1Ghz+ CPU and a dedicated sound and graphics card. So, the main challenge (still today) is to get good and stable performance to get a smooth quality user experience the game deserves.

To overcome this challenge, I need to understand the complete inner workings and identify all the critical data structures, routines and dependencies. Step by step I have recoded all these critical parts, using Apollo specific features both from C++ level as well as in a lot of dedicated developed Apollo ASM routines.

Time will tell if I (with the help of my teammates) will succeed in getting Robin Hood to a quality level that is sufficient for any form of release. Remember that the whole project is started as an internal and personal experiment, and I never expected to get as far as I have at this point.

8. Will the port run on the V2 or only the V4 series?

Given the challenge described in the previous question, I would already be very happy to get the game running smooth on V4. I very much doubt if it will be possible to make this happen for V2, but I also do not rule out anything at this point.

9. How has the advanced Vampire hardware made the game easier to port?

Apollo 68080 architecture is clean and simple and yet very powerful for developers using familiar 68k style registers and instructions. For example I could relatively easy rewrite the complete Video and Audio parts of Robin which were based on the fine but bulky SDL framework. Dozens of complex Audio routines for handling starting/stopping/mixing channels were replaced by new code with only a few lines and all critical Graphic routines were replaced by short ASM routines which typically take no more than 25 instructions or so. Within my scope of skills and experience this could only be done on the Apollo V4 platform.

10. What is the current stage of the game's development?

It’s fair to say that we are beyond our "experimental" level, and I believe a "beta" stage would be the appropriate qualification. There is still some work to do in squeezing out extra FPS to really make Robin and all his friend come fully to live on the V4’s of our growing and loyal Apollo community. Also, the game profile manager which handles save games and such is still a pain in the lower part of my body (Never Endian Story). Finally, all our development testing is done in the one "demo level" and I kind of expect some bumps in the road when we start travelling through a grand total of no less than 16 complex Missions.

11. When do you expect the game to be released?

It’s still too early to give any indication for that. Amiga community is full of promises, but sadly a lot of them are no more than that. I would like to take the time needed to finish something properly.

12. What game would you like to create or port next for the Amiga platform?

After Robin I will likely take a pause from Game coding/porting and work more on ApolloBoot and ApolloOS. But in the longer run it would be logical to look at Desperados as this is the "twin" from Robin. Also, I have an idea to develop a simple but fun Game fully coded in ASM. I already have the graphics for this, designed by Apollo Team friend Kevin Saunders.

13. For Amiga users interested in buying a Vampire card or StandAlone system, what advice do you have for them?

Don`t hesitate and just make the step. Apollo V4 Stand Alone as well as the V4 IceDrake (A1200), V4 FireBird (A500/1000/2000) and V4 MantiCore (A600) accelerators will bring you into a revived Amiga world where you can enjoy both the retro history as well as exciting new developments which come from our very active Team and Community. Visit our Discord server and become part of our little crazy, but always positive group of Amiga fans.

14. Thank you for your time. Do you have any final words before we wrap up?

Again, it is me thanking you and your colleagues for spending precious time on creating WhatIFF magazine and keeping us all informed on all the interesting Amiga stuff. I truly believe that the Amiga community can become more positive and cohesive by means of better communication and information. This is why initiatives like WhatIFF magazine are important. Regarding questions or chats on Robin Hood, ApolloBoot, ApolloOS and other things I am to be found on Apollo Discord (@willemdrijver). Thank you all for reading and have fun with Amiga in whatever shape or form is most suitable for you.

Cheers!

### Interview mit [_Fabian Del Priore_](http://www.delpriore.de)

Nachdem es nun seit längerem nichts Neues zu berichten gab bin ich nun, wie bereits erwähnt durch Zufall auf Fabian Del Priore aufmerksam geworden, der sich auf diese Website "verirrt" hat. Er ist Komponist für Chicago 1930 und damit der Nachfolger des allseits bekannten Tapio "Shao-Lee" Muley. In Anlehnung an das Interview mit Tapio, geführt von unserer Partnerseite [robinhood-game.de](http://www.robinhood-game.de) habe ich nun ein ähnliches mit Fabian "zusammengeschustert".

**chicago1930.de.vu:**_Wie bist du eigentlich dazu gekommen Musik für Computerspiele zu komponieren ?

_**Fabian Del Priore** Ich habe 1995 Musik mit meinen Synthesizern im Studio komponiert und ein Demotape an Chris Hülsbeck geschickt, der damals ebenfalls in Frankfurt lebte und arbeitete (in Langen bei Frankfurt). Bald darauf erhielt ich eine positive Antwort und wurde in sein Studio eingeladen. Nach dem Vorstellungsgespräch kristallisierte sich heraus, dass ich in nächster Zeit Aufträge vertonen würde. Damals noch kleine Spielchen, aber mit "Extreme Assault" (1997, Bluebyte) ist mir schon ein größerer Coup gelungen - das war eins der bekanntesten Spiele zu der Zeit überhaupt (zumindest in Deutschland). Danach und dadurch bekam ich immer größere Aufträge und der Rest ist Geschichte...

**chicago1930.de.vu:**_Welche Spiele zockst du persönlich am liebsten, oder spielst du nicht gerne ? Wie sieht es mit den "Chicago 1930" Vorgängern "Robin Hood - Die Legende von Sherwood" und "Desperados - Wanted Dead or Alive" aus ?

_**Fabian Del Priore** Die habe ich natürlich im Vorfeld gespielt und mir angesehen, allein schon deswegen um mich für Chicago 1930 zu inspirieren, das war nützlich... Persönlich zocke ich Jump'n'Runs (wie Mario Sunshine, GC), Action Adventure / RPGs im Stile von Zelda Wind Waker (GameCube) und Drakan 2 (PS2) am liebsten. Autorennen wie Gran Turismo 3 haben es mir auch angetan, sowie Survival Horror à la Dino Crisis (Playstation). Auf dem PC spiele ich allerdings nur selten spiele, da man mit der Leistung des PCs nach 2 Wochen wieder hoffnungslos hinterherhinkt, das mache ich nicht mit ;-) Da lobe ich mir Konsolenspiele, da die Konsolen nicht so schnell "geupdated" werden müssen wie die PC Hardware.. Auf dem PC spiele ich aber liebend gerne Emulatorenspiele, z.B. C64 Spiele, etc., daddel nostalgische Arcade Games oder auch Super Nintendo und Mega Drive Spiele (deren Spiele ich alle heute noch von damals besitze - ist also legal! ;-)

**chicago1930.de.vu:**_Hast du auch die Hintergrundmusik im Spiel komponiert ?

_**Fabian Del Priore** Die Hintergrundmusik habe ich auch gemacht, ja.. die ganze Musik im Spiel eben.. und einige der Soundeffekte die man hört.

**chicago1930.de.vu:**_Kennst du auch Tapio Muley, den Komponisten von "Robin Hood - Die Legende von Sherwood" ?

_**Fabian Del Priore** Ja, Tapio kenne ich, und seine Musik für Robin Hood ist wirklich gut.

**chicago1930.de.vu:**_Kennst du Tapio nur vom Hören-Sagen, hast du ihn schon mal getroffen oder seid ihr gar Konkurrenten ?

_**Fabian Del Priore** Ich kenne ihn eigentlich über Spellbound, da er ja der Robin Hood Musiker war. Persönlich habe ich ihn noch nicht getroffen, wird sich aber evtl. demnächst ändern, wenn wir uns bei Spellbound mal treffen...

**chicago1930.de.vu:**_Wie ist es eigentlich zur Zusammenarbeit mit Spellbound gekommen?

_**Fabian Del Priore** Tapio kannte meine bisherige Arbeit und hat mich bei Spellbound weiterempfohlen. Spellbound hat sich dann auch letztendlich für mich entschieden, was die Vertonung von Chicago 1930 anbelangt. Die Arbeit mit Spellbound hat mir sehr gut gefallen, es ist ein klasse eingespieltes Team und alle sind sehr nett, es hat mir einfach viel Spaß gemacht, mit ihnen zu arbeiten und wird es hoffentlich auch in Zukunft weiterhin! Chicago 1930 war ein sehr interessantes und sehr gutes Projekt.

**chicago1930.de.vu:**_Wie fallen einem solche schönen Hintergrundmusiken ein? Hast dir auch wie Tapio Muley Ausdrucke von den Levels gemacht und dich davon inspirieren lassen ?

_**Fabian Del Priore** Ich habe viele Screenshots von Spellbound bekommen und auch die Videosequenzen haben mich enorm inspiriert, sowie einige Vorschlagsmusiken, die wir im Vorfeld durchgegangen sind. Auch in den Meetings in Kehl habe ich die aktuellen Versionen des Spiels anschauen können und mich weiter inspiriert. Ich weiss auch nicht genau, wie mir die Hintergrundmusiken einfallen, ich spiele ein wenig auf einem Synthesizer, stelle mir die düstere gangsterartige Atmosphäre vor, neblige und dunkle Gassen voll von Ganoven, die nichts gutes im Schilde führen, etc... Dann kommt das ganz von selbst.

**chicago1930.de.vu:**_Du hast ja auch den Sound der Videos in Chicago gemacht: Wird es wieder nur zwei gerenderte Zwischensequenzen geben oder hat Wanadoo bei Chicago die Spendierhosen angehabt ?

_**Fabian Del Priore** Es wird 2 Shortros (Kurze Renderfilme, richtig) geben, die ich vertont habe, dazu Intro und Outro. Ich habe noch verschiedene Jingles (Mission gewonnen, verloren etc) vertont, es steht zu vermuten, dass dazu ebenfalls Renderfilme gezeigt werden....

**chicago1930.de.vu:**_Willst du den Fans von Chicago 1930 zum Abschluss noch irgendwas sagen ?

_**Fabian Del Priore** Don't drink and drive - and enjoy the game (hopefully the music as well!) ;-)

**chicago1930.de.vu:**_Nochmal Danke für das informative Interview_
