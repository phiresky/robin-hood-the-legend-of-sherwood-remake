  The Martin Kuppe Interview - 27 August 2002 17:53 - Paligap User Comments : 1
 

Paligap: Background in gaming (please also give us your full name and title on Robin Hood – maybe a brief description of your daily duties on the title)

Martin Kuppe: I am one of two game designers at Spellbound who are in charge of the overall game design of Robin Hood and our other products. The other guy is Jean-Marc Haessig, our Creative Director. I am an artist and he is a mathematician - this cooperation works wery well. Furthermore I am programmer: I wrote the artificial intelligence of Robin Hood. In addition I have made the level design for some levels.
Before this project I worked on Desperados - Wanted Dead or Alive, where I also coded the AI and designed some levels.

What is the plot or story to this version of Robin Hood?

Martin Kuppe: The plot goes like this: prince Leopold of Austria is holding King Richard Lionheard for ransom. At the same time his evil brother John Lackland tries to seize the crown and oppress the people with the aid of the Sheriff of Nottingham. Robin of Locksley, chased from his castle by the sheriff, opposes the prince and his henchmen. He hides in Sherwood Forest, finds a group of outlaws and begins to collect the ransom for himself – mainly by attacking convoys in the forest and robbing gold in the cities. He contacts two royalist barons and organizes the resistance against Prince John. The prince is not amused, he orders his barons to attack the castles of the royalists. But with the aid of Robin and his merry men, the royalists defend themselves and begin the counter attack...

Is it more inline with the original story or the “kiddy version” Disney put out?

Martin Kuppe: The story is inspired by the original ballads, but also by the later traditions up to modern movies, which are now an indispensable part of the legend. Remember that if we would only follow the five original ballads, there would be no Maid Marian, no king Richard, no quarterstaff fight, even no “take from the rich and give to the poor” – at least not the “give to the poor” part. But I can assure you that the story has nothing to do with the Disney version – for the simple reason that it’s me who wrote the story, and I have never seen the Disney film. :o)

What do you think the hardest part is working on a title that has a main character recognized by all ages throughout the world?

Martin Kuppe: The main problem is that everybody has his own idea of the main characters. This is even more valid for the secondary characters like Maid Marian or Little John, as they are less “standardized” than Robin himself. Friar Tuck, for example, is small and thick in our game, although many people may imagine him tall and strong. It is simply not possible to match the ideas of all people – unless you add a “Friar Tuck is tall and strong” button in the options. ;o)

What are some of the different areas we will visit – Sherwood Forest obviously but any other “famous” Robin Hood places?

Martin Kuppe: Besides the HQ in Sherwood forest and the three forest crossroads, the player can visit five towns and castles: Lincoln, Leicester, Derby, the city of York and, of course, Nottingham. All towns and castles exist in different ambiences - good weather, fog, night – which also influence the gameplay, as the enemies see less far in fog or night.

How many levels will there be in the game? Is that how it is split up? Levels or areas?

Martin Kuppe: The game is composed of 37 missions that are played in the above-mentioned 8 locations. The missions are arranged in nonlinear order. Between the missions you find yourself in your HQ in Sherwood Forest. There you can assign team members to tasks like making arrows or bow training, select the next mission on a map and compose your mission team before the next mission is loaded.

Is it mission-based? If so, what are some of the missions?

Martin Kuppe: There are many different types of missions. Some examples:

  • Sneak into the castle of a good royalist baron to contact him, without killing any soldiers.
  • Save Stuteley and some peasants from the stocks
  • Participate at an archers contest at Nottingham that is, in fact, a trap set by the sheriff.
  • Prevent the forced wedding of Maid Marian and the evil Guy of Guisbourne.
  • Spy out a secret meeting between Prince John and his barons
  • Intercept a tax transport or an escort on a forest crossroad, by using many different traps prepared by the Merry Men. This kind of mission exists in many variants that are mainly how the player collects the ransom for King Richard.
  • Intercept a food or weapon transport to weaken the enemy forces before a battle
  • Introduce into an enemy castle during a battle in order to help the attacking royalist forces by opening a gate, disabling a squad of elite archers, lowering a drawbridge, killing an enemy general etc. In these kinds of missions, you can call allied soldiers for help.

When announced I pictured a Diablo-type game where you go from town to town “robbing the rich and giving to the poor” – is that accurate?

Martin Kuppe: Robin Hood is more a team-based infiltration game like Desperados or Commandos, with different special actions for every player character. When you start a mission, you find your mission team in a coin of the map and you can plan how to procede. The focus is not just on collecting gold and killing adversaries, but on reaching the mission objectives that are different from mission to mission – see above.
But action is not neglected. We added an easy-to-handle swordfight system – you make special strikes simply by drawing mouse traces on the screen. In fact you can always choose whether you prefer infiltration and sophisticated plans or swordfight action.
An important point is that the game rewards players who spare the lifes of their enemies. The less soldiers to kill in a mission, the more merry men will join your band afterwards.
