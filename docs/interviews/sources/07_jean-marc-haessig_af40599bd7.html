Robin Hood - The Legend of Sherwood Interview
August 21, 2002


Returning to England from the Crusades, King Richard the Lionheart has been taken prisoner. Held in an Austrian castle, his chances of escape or rescue are virtually non-existent. Of course, it will be possible to gain his freedom by paying a ransom, but the amount demanded by his captors is exorbitant. Neither is the regent, Prince John, eager to pay it. In his brother's absence, he has grown very accustomed to the power of the throne, and together with his supporters, a number of powerful Norman barons, he is actively lining his pockets through oppressive taxation, the proclamation of unjust laws and various other thinly veiled unscrupulous activities. The people have little reason for hope, but deep in the heart of Sherwood Forest north of Nottingham, one man and his followers refuse to bend and continue to live free.

Now late in development at Spellbound Studios, Robin Hood - The Legend of Sherwood will allow you to assume the role of the legendary hero, leading Little John, Friar Tuck, other members of the Merry Men band and Lady Marian in a fight for the common people, against the seemingly overwhelming forces of the tyrant. Described as a game of tactics and action, it will take the protagonists on an adventure through locations ranging from the famed forest to Nottingham Castle, York et al armed with bows and arrows, swords and their own steely resolve. Recently, we were pleased to receive the opportunity to learn a lot more about it in this extensive Robin Hood - The Legend of Sherwood Interview with Creative Director Jean-Marc Haessig of Spellbound and Producer Anne Devouassoux of European publisher Wanadoo.


Action Vault: To take care of the preliminaries, please introduce yourselves and outline your respective roles on Robin Hood.

Jean-Marc Haessig: I'm the Creative Director of Robin Hood at Spellbound. I'm in charge of the overall game design and the look and feel of our products.

Anne Devouassoux: I'm the Robin Hood producer at Wanadoo. I'm in charge of the overall quality of the game content and gameplay.

Action Vault: What type of game is Robin Hood, and what kind of gameplay does it focus on? What genres, blend of genres or individual games have been influential in the game's design or development?

Jean-Marc Haessig: Robin Hood will be a real-time tactics game like Desperados and Commandos, taking place in 12th century England. The idea is to provide real-time tactics fans an alternative to the World War II setting seen in so many other games in this genre. Although Robin Hood is a famous character throughout the world, there haven't been many games that deal with his legend in the past.

The player will have to do a lot of things. Sneak into mighty castles, ambush tax collectors in Sherwood Forest, figure out strategies to solve the missions (including who will join the team in a mission) and so on. The plot will be based on the legend of Robin Hood. The main objective of the game is to gather enough gold to ensure the safe return of King Richard.

Action Vault: What is the basic structure of the game? How are the missions and objectives laid out? Does the player have to follow a linear sequence or will there be choices to make?

Jean-Marc Haessig: The starting point for every mission is a map showing all the locations of the game. There will be several missions available at any given time, both story missions and random missions. In random missions, for example, the player's goal will be to rob a tax collector or weapons transport - both earning him money. In story missions, not only does the story advance, but he will also be able to collect more money through chests or moneybags. After all, getting the money for King Richard's return is the main object.

Given that there will be story missions and random missions available at the same time, the player will have to make the difficult choice of what to do next.

Action Vault: What attracted you to create a game, and in particular a tactical one, based on the Robin Hood theme? And what are the main factors that have influenced the project?

Jean-Marc Haessig: Robin Hood and his Merry Men are the ideal characters for a real-time tactics game. His name is known by virtually everyone around the world, and almost everybody knows at least some of the tales that are told about him. They're a small band of Merry Men in a struggle against the evil Prince John Lackland and his minions. Robin is not a great leader of a large army inside Sherwood Forest who fight an all out war against the Normans. His goals and methods are quite "subtle" - stealing taxes from Prince John, giving back the money and saving some of it for King Richard's ransom. And he achieves all of that with a small band of Merry Men. Then again, would Robin Hood have succeeded if he hadn't been accompanied by his Merry Men? If he couldn't count on the strength of Little John or the faith - and beer - of Friar Tuck, or the love of Maid Marian? We think that what makes the legend of Robin Hood so imaginative and popular is the wide range of characters it includes.

So when Wanadoo approached us with their concept for a Robin Hood game, we both put our ideas together and quickly had a game to show.

Research is always a big issue when designing a new game. There were a lot of things to learn -for the graphic artists as well as for our level and story designers. Our sources included the Internet, books on Robin Hood, medieval architecture and clothing, film adaptations like The Adventures of Robin Hood with Errol Flynn and last but not least Strasbourg, the city nearest to us. Its medieval town center offered inspiration for our 3D modellers who designed the houses and castles in Robin Hood. Finally, the cities and castles in Robin Hood are based on original maps and pictures of York, Derby, Nottingham, Leicester and Lincoln, but have been strongly adapted to meet our gameplay priorities.

Action Vault: Since you've listed a number of the locations, how many will there be in total? And what were your most important goals when choosing and creating them?

Jean-Marc Haessig: The missions will be set in the towns and castles surrounding Sherwood Forest: Nottingham, York, Lincoln, Leicester and Derby and of course in Sherwood itself. All in all there will be nine places to visit, some of them more than once and under different background conditions - day, night, fog, snow...

Our objective was to create strong identities for each location, to avoid confusion between the different castles, and also to provide more excitement and fun. So we have used all the known elements of medieval castles, towns and landscapes and have adjusted them to obtain a balance between gameplay, atmosphere and realism.
