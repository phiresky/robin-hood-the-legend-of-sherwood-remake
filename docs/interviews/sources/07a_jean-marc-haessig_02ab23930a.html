Robin Hood - The Legend of Sherwood Interview
August 21, 2002


Action Vault: Which of the famous characters will the player control and how will they differ from each other? And will there also be a variety of other Merry Men? And can any or all of the player characters improve during the game?

Jean-Marc Haessig: There are six heroes and five types of Merry Men, all with their own characteristics. For example, the heroic Robin Hood is unquestionably the best archer in the game, but he is equally skilled at sword and staff fighting, as well as hand-to-hand combat. He is the most agile character and only he can fight duels with characters such as the Sheriff of Nottingham... and he can play on the enemy soldiers' greed by tempting them with purses of silver.

Little John fights only with a staff, though his great strength doubles its power. He can lift soldiers in armor up over his head, help his friends climb hard to reach places, push heavy objects such as tree trunks, and knock out his enemies like no one else. His piercing whistle is particularly useful for drawing soldiers to him.

Friar Tuck is the company's resident glutton! With him, any opportunity for having a quick snack is never wasted. He is also in charge of transporting the company's beer, which he uses to get enemy soldiers drunk. His favorite weapon is a mace, which leaves his victims with a worse hangover than any drink will. His natural caution has him tying up anyone he knocks down. His favorite trick is to throw a wasps' nest down a soldier's suit of armor!

Maid Marian is prepared to do anything to defend her country and help Robin. She is a skilled archer and swords-woman and is also well versed in the healing powers of plants. But her most precious gift is the ability to determine the identity of any soldier whose voice she hears, even if he happens to be on the other side of a wall! Like Robin, she never fails to search any corpse she comes across for money to put towards paying King Richard's ransom.

Will Scarlet is Robin's nephew, a young, happy go lucky character eager for action. His weapon of choice is a deadly flail and he doesn't balk at finishing off wounded soldiers - or at sneaking up on them and strangling them. He carries a large shield, which he can use to protect himself and another member of the company from enemy arrows. He is very agile and his prowess with the catapult enables him to strike enemies from a distance.

Stuteley is an old friend of Robin's. He is an expert at setting traps and ambushes and nobody can match his ability to capture netloads of enemy soldiers. He fights with a hatchet and also throws apples to knock out his opponents or else make them think that the local children are playing tricks on them. What's more, the apples only serve to attract Friar Tuck's wasps! One of his favorite tricks is disguising himself as a beggar and mingling with the unsuspecting soldiers...

The Merry Men are made up of local peasants who join Robin in Sherwood, bringing with them makeshift weapons such as pitchforks and clubs... They share some of the heroes' abilities but, unlike them, they have the advantage of being replaceable... Some can be sent out on missions while others remain behind in Sherwood producing weapons and food. Still others can be trained in the fine art of ambushing enemy soldiers. All the Merry Men are skilled at archery and hand to hand combat. Over the course of the game, their abilities evolve in keeping with the training they undergo and the battles they win.

Action Vault: How did you choose the different weapons for the various characters? And can players expect to see more varieties than those you just mentioned?

Jean-Marc Haessig: Each character has his own weapons, which we allotted in keeping with the well-known traits of the Robin Hood story. For example, Robin fights with a wooden staff in the forest and a sword elsewhere - he also keeps his bow and arrows with him at all times. Marian uses a bow and a sword. Little John only uses a staff, but his physical strength doubles its force. Will Scarlet is the most brutal member of the team and he uses a flail. Friar Tuck uses a mace while Stutely uses a hatchet. The other Merry Men use swords, pitchforks, clubs... Add the spears, halberds, two-handed swords and crossbows used by the soldiers and you have quite a collection of deadly weapons.

Action Vault: What range of enemies will the player face, especially those drawn from the legend? What were your key goals when designing them and the AI that controls their behavior?

Jean-Marc Haessig: Aside from key villains such as the Sheriff of Nottingham, Guy of Gisborne, Longchamp, etc. we also came up with a whole series of enemy soldiers. The soldiers all have different traits based on their roles, from simple foot soldiers to noble cavalrymen, and taking in lancers, halberdiers, archers, crossbowmen, officers and lords. What's more, each type of soldier exists in five variations, the weakest are dressed in blue while the elite wear black. Those in between wear yellow, orange and red. Those dressed in green are Robin's allies and fight by his side.

Our aim was to create an army with a credible, coherent hierarchy between officers and soldiers. It also includes more independent characters such as lords - these are proud individuals and won't join in battle with simple soldiers! Instead, they will observe the battle from a distance, only joining in when there are no soldiers left. Officers will summon soldiers in order to give them orders, and foot soldiers armed with shields will form lines to protect the archers behind them. The game's AI was entirely reworked in order to recreate the battle tactics of the time, such as sword, mace and hand to hand fighting!

Action Vault: Will friendly or neutral NPCs play important roles in Robin Hood? And are there any other famous characters that will appear?

Jean-Marc Haessig: You will encounter friendly NPCs all through the game, from defenceless peasants needing help (and earning you bonuses) to beggars who will provide information in return for small sums of money. Most of the legend's key characters, such as Ranulph or Sir Godwin, play an important part in the plot, as do Robin's sworn enemies - the Sheriff of Nottingham, Prince John, Guy of Gisborne, etc.

Action Vault: While we touched on the topic earlier, please elaborate on the game's missions and your primary goals when designing them. How many are there, and how much do they vary in types and goals?

Jean-Marc Haessig: Our aim is to provide as much diversity as possible over a solid gameplay base. We want to surprise the player and give him as much pleasure as possible. We therefore decided not to simply have the missions follow one after another, in a linear manner, but to create a real campaign with a map of the area around Sherwood showing castles and paths through the forest. Players can choose between several different missions at any given moment and can even select different teams for different missions. Campaigns are made up of about 40 missions and include rescue missions for recruiting new Merry Men, spying missions for finding out Prince John's plans, ambush missions for robbing the tax collectors, siege missions for attacking castles, missions for attacking military convoys passing through the forest and even a mission to prevent the marriage of Maid Marian to the despicable Gisborne!
