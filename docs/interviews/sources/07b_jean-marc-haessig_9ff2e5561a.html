Robin Hood - The Legend of Sherwood Interview
August 21, 2002


Action Vault: How are the different mission types reflected in the level or area designs? And what degree of world interactivity will there be - NPC interactions, objects to find and use et al?

Jean-Marc Haessig: There are story missions designed to advance the game's plot; these include rescue, spying and infiltration missions. There are also lots of mini-missions for earning bonuses and money, strategic missions involving the siege or defence of a castle, ambush missions in the forest for recapturing taxes collected by the Sheriff, and missions involving the interception of enemy troops. Interactivity with the environment is very wide-ranging, from opening a drawbridge to knocking over a pile of wood, from setting traps in the forest with nets to climbing ivy-covered walls, as well as the bonuses and information hidden throughout the game's large maps.
Interaction with the enemy is of course more exciting, thanks to hand to hand combat against one or more opponents, and the various ways of killing, knocking out, trapping and generally annoying your enemies...

Action Vault: Will Robin Hood include scripted events to advance the plot? Will there be many, and what degree of importance will they have overall?

Jean-Marc Haessig: Generally, scripted events will only be found at the start of missions, in order to set the tone and provide links with the previous missions. However, as unexpected events can suddenly occur in the middle of a mission, there will be some scripted events designed to surprise players.

Action Vault: What engine are you using, and what makes it especially suited to the game? What major modifications and changes are you making, and what will the system requirements and recommendations for Robin Hood be?

Jean-Marc Haessig: We took the motor used for Desperados as our starting point as it allowed for high levels of detail. We modified and improved it to make passages between missions shorter, the animation more fluid and the AI specifically adapted to this environment. We used a new data compression system to produce larger, more detailed characters and backgrounds. Transition animations link one action to another, making character movements very smooth. We've improved the game interface, and also the quick-actions that were so popular with Desperados; this time round, players will be able to program up to three quick-actions per team member...

The minimum required configuration will be a 500Mhz processor, 128MB of RAM and about 1GB of free disk space.

Action Vault: The game doesn't seem to include multiplayer as a feature? Why did you choose not to incorporate it?

Jean-Marc Haessig: No, there is no multiplayer mode. As in Desperados, the genre doesn't justify such a mode.

Action Vault: When and how did the idea for the game initially arise? When did actual development begin, and what is the current status? When will Robin Hood be ready for release, and how confident are you about this date?

Anne Devouassoux: The idea arose as we played Desperados Wanted Dead or Alive and Commandos. Many people around us were indeed fans of those two hits, and we had a lot of fun playing those games. At the same time, we were thinking about developing a game based on the theme of Robin Hood. So naturally, we mixed our pleasure for tactical games and our will to work in this universe, and decided to begin designing the concept of Robin Hood and defining its main features. We contacted Spellbound Studio and proposed the Robin Hood concept to them. They were really excited because the concept happened to correspond with the new concept they were currently working on!

The pre-production began last September. The pre-production and production phases are very short because the Robin Hood engine is based on the Desperados engine, with new features of course. The production cycle is 12 months long.

The game is presently at pre-master candidate, so I'm very confident in an end of the year release.

Action Vault: Please tell our readers about Spellbound and its background. When and how was the Robin Hood team put together and where is it located? Who are the key members, and what backgrounds do you have making games?

Jean-Marc Haessig: Spellbound was founded six years ago in Kehl, on the French-German border. Initially, we designed games for the German market and our first international title was Airline Tycoon. We then spent two years working on Desperados, increasing our team from four to 16 in the process. For Robin Hood, we increased our team to 22 people, allowing us to complete the game in a record 10 months. We're currently ironing out the bugs in the game and, of course, developing new projects.

Most of our team cut their teeth on Desperados, and our most senior programmer is Armin Gessert who has been programming since 1984 and who created Great Giana Sisters on the C64!

Action Vault: Why will Robin Hood stand out from other action-oriented tactical games of the present time? What kind of gamer will find it most appealing?

Anne Devouassoux: Our goal during development was fun and no frustration for the gamer. We analysed other games of this genre and put in new features like non-linear campaigns, combat mode, collection of items, several different kinds of missions, etc.

It's really up to the player to choose the way he wants to play. If he wants to play in a "classical way" (like he plays Commandos or Desperados), he can. If he wants to play in an action way, rather than a tactical way, he can. If he wants to let the AI manage the combat, he can. If he prefers to play the combat, he can. Hardcore gamers will play exactly the same way they are used to. And new players will enjoy the game because it's not necessary to be an experienced tactical/action gamer to have fun with Robin Hood.

The gameplay is a subtle mix between hardcore gameplay and "obviousness".

Action Vault: Is there anything else you'd like to tell our readers, or something you'd like to ask them?

Jean-Marc Haessig: Once again, we've done our best to top our previous efforts and to give players as many hours of fun as possible. With three difficulty levels, we hope to be able to reach more players. We want players to get the full value for their money and with this in mind we take their ideas and comments into consideration - after all, nothing is as important as true emotion.

Spellbound Studios seems poised to deliver a memorable title combining the lengthy list of gameplay elements and features enumerated herein and spiced with familiar characters in a compelling story. Our thanks to Jean-Marc Haessig and Anne Devouassoux for very informative introduction to their game and to North American publisher Strategy First for arranging it. And of course, stay tuned to Action vault for further information over the months to come.

Richard Aihoshi - "Jonric"
