Robin Hood: The Legend Of Sherwood Interview - 13 August 2002 00:02 - John [JCal] Callaham User Comments : 1
 

The legend of Robin Hood and his band of Merry Men stealing from the rich in medieval England and giving their goods to the poor has been told and retold countless times over the centuries and in many different formats. Later this year developer Spellbound Studios (best known for their Infogrames Wild West action-strategy game Desperados) will give their version of the legend in Robin Hood: The Legend Of Sherwood, their upcoming action-strategy title. HomeLAN got a chance to chat with Spellbound's creative director Jean-Marc Haessig along with Anne Devouassoux, the game's producer at Wanadoo, to find out more about Robin Hood which will be published in the US by Strategy First.


HomeLAN - How did the idea for developing a Robin Hood game come about?

Anne Devouassoux - The idea arised as we played Desperados “Wanted Dead or Alive” and Commandos. Many people around us were indeed fans of those two hits, we had a lot of fun playing those games. At the same time, we were thinking about developing a game on the theme of Robin Hood. Even if the legend of Robin Hood took place in the 14th century the theme is still very appealing and has great brand recognition.

So naturally, we mixed our pleasure for tactical games and our will to work in this universe, and decided to begin designing the concept of the game Robin Hood and to define its main features. Obviously we contacted the Spellbound Studio and asked them to work on the concept of Robin Hood. They were really excited because that concept happened to be similar to the new concept they were already working on!

HomeLAN - There are many different game genres that could have been used for Robin Hood. Why did you decide to create the game as a tactical-action title?

Jean-Marc Haessig - Robin Hood and his “Merry Men” are the ideal characters for a real-time tactical game. His name is known by virtually everyone around the world and almost everybody knows of at least some of the tales that are told about him. They’re a small band of “Merry Men” in a struggle against the evil Prince John Lackland and his minions. Robin is a great leader of a large army inside Sherwood Forest who fights an all out war against the Normans. His goals and methods are quite “subtle” – stealing taxes from Prince John, giving back the money and saving some of it for King Richard’s ransom. And he achieves all of that with a small band of “Merry Men”. Then again, would Robin Hood have succeeded if he hadn’t been accompanied by his merry men? If he couldn’t count on the strength of Little John or the faith – and beer – of Friar Tuck or the love of Maid Marian? We think that’s what makes the legend of Robin Hood so imaginative and popular is the wide range of characters it includes.
So when Wanadoo approached us with their concept for a Robin Hood game, we both put our ideas together and very soon had a game to show.

Anne Devouassoux - The strength of the theme is the personality of the leader (Robin Hood) and his group (the merry men). It was really important for us to play Robin Hood as well as the other characters too and the theme fits with the different missions proposed by a tactical/action game (stealth, ambush mission, save people, attack of castles, etc).

HomeLAN - How much research on the Robin Hood legend and on the game's time period did the team do?

Jean-Marc Haessig - Research is always a big issue when designing a new game. There were a lot of things to learn – for the graphic artists as well as for our level and story designers. Our sources included the Internet, books on Robin Hood, medieval architecture and clothing, film adaptations like “The Adventures of Robin Hood” with Errol Flynn and last but not least Strasbourg, the city nearest to us. Its medieval town center offered quite a few inspirations for our 3D-modellers who designed the houses and castles in Robin Hood.

Finally, the cities and castles in Robin Hood are based on original maps and pictures of York, Derby, Nottingham, Leicester and Lincoln, but have been strongly adapted to meet our gameplay priorities.

HomeLAN - What are some of your favorite interpretations of the Robin Hood legend?

Jean-Marc Haessig - I’m a big fan of the Errol Flynn film – I like its energy, color and the character styles. Kevin Costner’s film took a more modern approach, which is also very interesting, though I found that, aside from Robin and Will, the other characters were a bit two-dimensional… In any case, I liked almost all of the versions of the Robin Hood legend, from cartoons such as Walt Disney to cameos such as Mickey’s or even the one in Shrek, etc. Robin Hood is a very strong, symbolic character who appears and reappears in different guises throughout our lives. We’re happy to be able to provide our own interpretation of the character and proud to contribute to the Legend of Robin Hood.

HomeLAN - What is the actual storyline for the game?

Jean-Marc Haessig - We’ve made some changes to the true Legend of Robin Hood, both to provide surprises for those familiar with it and to suit the needs of the gameplay. Overall, though, our version contains all the main elements of the Legend, starting with Robin’s return from the Crusades and ending with his duel with the Sheriff of Nottingham, taking in his encounter with the Merry Men, the archery tournament, Marian’s marriage to Guy of Gisborne, etc., along the way.

One example of the changes we’ve made is Robin’s meeting with Friar Tuck. In the Legend, they meet on the banks of a stream and Robin asks Friar Tuck to carry him across the water on his back. In the game, Tuck appears twice before he joins the team, and he is also to be found – strangely enough – on top of a keep…

HomeLAN - What locations will be featured in Robin Hood?

Jean-Marc Haessig - The missions will be set in the towns and castles surrounding Sherwood Forest: Nottingham, York, Lincoln, Leicester and Derby and of course in Sherwood itself. All in all there will be nine places to visit, some of them more than once and under different background conditions (day, night, fog, snow…).

There are also encounters in Sherwood Forest which allow Robin’s men to attack tax collectors and garrisons of enemy soldiers.

HomeLAN - What playable characters will be in the game and what will their abilities be?

Jean-Marc Haessig - There are 6 heroes and 5 types of Merry Men, all with their own characteristics.

For example, the heroic Robin Hood is unquestionably the best archer in the game, but he is equally skilled at sword and staff fighting, as well as hand-to-hand combat. He is the most agile character and only he can fight duels with characters such as the Sheriff of Nottingham… He can play on the enemy soldiers’ greed by tempting them with purses of silver.

Little John fights only with a staff, though his great strength doubles its power. He can lift soldiers in armor up over his head, help his friends climb up to hard to reach places, push heavy objects such as tree trunks, and knock out his enemies like no-one else. His piercing whistle is particularly useful for drawing soldiers to him.

Friar Tuck is the company’s resident glutton! With him, any opportunity for having a quick snack is never wasted. He is also in charge of transporting the company’s beer, which he uses to get enemy soldiers drunk… His favorite weapon is a mace which leaves his victims with a worse hangover than any drink will. His natural caution also causes him to tie up anyone he knocks out.. His favorite trick is to throw a wasps’ nest down a soldier’s suit of armor!

Maid Marian is prepared to do anything to defend her country and help Robin. She is a skilled archer and swords-woman and is also well versed in the healing powers of plants. But her most precious gift is the ability to determine the identity of any soldier whose voice she hears, even if he happens to be on the other side of a wall! Like Robin, she never fails to search any corpses she comes across for money to put towards paying King Richard’s ransom.

Will Scarlet is Robin’s nephew, a young happy-go-lucky character eager for action. His weapon of choice is a deadly flail and he doesn’t balk at finishing off wounded soldiers – or at sneaking up on them and strangling them. He carries a large shield which he can use to protect himself and another member of the company from enemy arrows. He is very agile and his prowess with the catapult enables him to strike enemies from a distance.

Stuteley is an old friend of Robin’s. He is an expert at setting traps and ambushes and nobody can match his ability to capture net-loads of enemy soldiers. He fights with a small ax and also throws apples to knock out his opponents or else make them think that the local children are playing tricks on them. What’s more, the apples only serve to attract Friar Tuck’s wasps! One of his favorite tricks is disguising himself as a beggar and mingling with the unsuspecting soldiers…

The Merry Men are made up of local peasants who join up with Robin in Sherwood, bringing with them makeshift weapons such as pitchforks and clubs… They share some of the heroes’ abilities but, unlike them, they have the advantage of being replaceable… Some can be sent out on missions while others remain behind in Sherwood producing weapons and food. Still others can be trained in the fine art of ambushing enemy soldiers. All the Merry Men are skilled at archery and hand to hand combat. Over the course of the game, their abilities evolve in keeping with the training they undergo and the battles they win.

HomeLAN - Who will be the main enemies in Robin Hood?

Jean-Marc Haessig - All the key villains from the Legend of Robin Hood such as the evil Sheriff of Nottingham, the lazy Prince John, the cruel Guy of Gisborne who wants to marry Maid Marian, as well as less well known characters such as Longchamp, Scathlock, the tax collectors and hordes of enemy soldiers.

HomeLAN - What kinds of missions will be featured in the game?

Jean-Marc Haessig - There are 3 different types of missions:


  • Robbery and Infiltration: here you must find your way into enemy castles in order to seize riches amassed by the Normans on the backs of the poor, free hostages, attend secret meetings, or prevent the marriage of Marian to the despicable Guy of Gisborne.
  • Castle Attack: here you must help the Saxons conquer an enemy-occupied castle. You must weaken strategic Norman posts in order to make an invasion by the Saxon army or Robin’s men possible.
  • Ambush: small missions in Sherwood in which you must stop the tax collector’s convoy by trapping and attacking its escort or weaken enemy troops on their way to sieges.

    • What you can expect are quite a lot of different mission types – from rescuing a captured man to ambushes in Sherwood Forest to big castle sieges, where Robin Hood has to complete some strategic goals before the attack can begin. The most important thing is that we leave most of the choices to the player. This freedom starts with the tutorials in Robin Hood’s camp in Sherwood and continues with the choosing of men to form groups for the missions.

    HomeLAN - How will combat be handled in Robin Hood?

    Jean-Marc Haessig - Because most of the fighting in Robin Hood is hand-to-hand combat rather then gunfights we decided to include a fighting system which allows more than just point and click on the enemy. By drawing a symbol with his mouse, the player’s characters will be able to perform some nice tricks like thrusts or sweeping blows against their opponents. Of course the fights can be left to the AI. But then… who would turn down the chance of fighting a villain on a torch-lit stairway?

    HomeLAN - What other unique gameplay elements will be featured in the game?

    Jean-Marc Haessig - We’ve made some innovations which will delight fans of the genre, such as the ‘shadow-men’ who move about on the map at the start of the game. Normally, in this type of game, all your enemies are visible on the map right from the start. Other games show nothing at all, just a fog indicating war. We’ve opted for a system which allows players tell there are enemies present, without allowing them make out the enemy’s identity, be he a simple soldier, a lord, an archer or just a civilian… Players will have to move their men close enough to actually see the other characters in order to find out who they are. But there are also other means of finding out: you can buy information from beggars, or climb up onto Little John’s shoulders in order to see further, or take advantage of Marian’s sixth sense which allows her guess the identity of enemies she can’t see… This new approach maintains the level of suspense while covering the map with anonymous characters.

    Another example is the very popular quick-actions from Desperados. These have been improved and now allow players to program up to three consecutive actions per character. Now your plans can be as machiavellian as you want!

    Other new developments include the possibility of choosing missions from a map of England, of putting together a different team for each mission, of producing food and arrows in Sherwood, of training for battle, allowing players improve their men’s abilities at archery and hand to hand combat, etc.

    HomeLAN - What can you tell us about the game's graphics?

    Jean-Marc Haessig - Our graphics artists have outdone themsleves! Our programmers improved the data compression and storage systems which allowed for more fluid character animations. The characters are also larger than those in Desperados. To give you an idea, we doubled the number of images for each character, giving more than a gigabyte of non-compressed data! The backgrounds are also more highly defined – you can even see the leaves moving on the trees… The only limit to the graphics engine is our artists’ imaginations, and the end result shows that they really enjoyed the freedom they had to express themselves, filling in every detail with love and passion.

    HomeLAN - At the moment, what is the status of the game's progress and when do you expect to complete the game?

    Jean-Marc Haessig - Production has finished and we’re now putting the finishing touches to it and ironing out bugs so the game will be perfect when it comes out in 3 months…

    HomeLAN - What's next on Spellbound's agenda following the completion of Robin Hood?

    Jean-Marc Haessig - We’re working on new ideas, based on different technologies, which we’ll be showing to our partners. We’re in the process of making prototypes and previews in order to let them judge the ideas’ potential for themselves. Of course, these projects are all still highly confidential but we hope to be able to announce them soon.

    HomeLAN - Finally, is there anything else you wish to say about the game?

    Jean-Marc Haessig - Once again, we’ve done our best to top our previous efforts and to give players as many hours of fun as possible. With 3 difficulty levels, we hope to be able to reach more players. We want players to get full value for their money and with this in mind we take their ideas and comments on board – after all, nothing is as important as true emotion.

    Anne Devouassoux - It’s really up to the player to choose the way he wants to play (non-linear campaigns, combat mode managed by AI or the player, tactical or action approach, etc). Our goal was to allow the player to have fun while not being frustrated and I think we’re accomplished that. Players will be able to decide this for themselved when the game comes out for Christmas 2002!
